## Modules

| Module | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Overview** | The page CleanMate opens on; read-only, nothing to tick or remove. **Smart Scan**: the last Smart Scan's date and what it found or what cleaning it freed (the menu bar item's line), or an invitation to scan before the first one; **Scan Now** opens Smart Scan and starts a scan, and while Smart Scan has results **Clean … Selected** (the size ticked) opens them to review and clean there. **Suggestions**: up to 5 places where space can be won next, largest first, each with its size (or count) and a button that opens the module where you choose what to remove; nothing is ever cleaned from the Overview. They come from what CleanMate already knows or reads cheaply in the background, so the Overview opens at once and they appear a moment later: each Smart Scan group where the last scan (under 30 days old) found junk not cleaned since (**Open Smart Scan** scrolls to that group and scans first when there are no results), the Trash, apps not opened for 3 months (from the Uninstaller's list; **Open Uninstaller** shows only those, largest first), unused Docker data (only while Docker runs; it is never started), Time Machine local snapshots (**Open Maintenance** goes to them) and, only with Full Disk Access (without it they are hidden and nothing is asked), old installers and downloads and the Trash on external drives. Less than 100 MB is not suggested; with nothing left: **No suggestions — your disk is in good shape.** They are read again with **Refresh** (⌘R), after a clean in any module, and when the Overview shows if they are over 10 minutes old. **Drives**: the startup disk, then each external drive mounted in /Volumes that is local and writable (network shares, read-only disk images and Time Machine backup disks are left out), with used / total, free space as Finder counts it (the same figure as the menu bar item) and a capacity bar; past 90 % used the bar turns orange (red past 95 %) with **Almost full**. **Free space over time**: a chart of the startup disk's free space over the last 30 days (hover a day for its date and free space) and a sentence on where it is heading: losing or gaining about so much a week (losing also says in how many weeks the disk is full at that rate) or stable. Free space is recorded once a day, the latest reading of the day kept, when CleanMate starts and when Smart Scan finishes a scan or a clean; the last 400 days are kept in `~/Library/Application Support/CleanMate/disk-history.tsv`. The chart shows after readings on two days. **Freed by CleanMate**: three cards with what was deleted in the last 30 days (the menu bar's figure), in total, and in the last cleanup with its date, from the activity log, and under them a bar chart of what was deleted on each of the last 30 days (hover a bar for its date and size); items moved to the Trash are not counted. The first time the Overview shows after CleanMate starts, the numbers count up and the drive bars and both charts grow into place (under a second, once; with **Reduce motion** on they only fade in); after **Refresh**, changed figures roll to their new value. **Space outside your folders**: space in use that no folder in the list explains, for information only (local snapshots are deleted in Maintenance). **Time Machine local snapshots**: how many copies of the disk Time Machine keeps on the Mac between backups, and from which dates to which, listed with `tmutil listlocalsnapshots /` (no administrator password); their size can't be measured without administrator rights, so none is shown. macOS deletes them by itself when it needs the space; **Delete…** opens Maintenance, where they can be deleted now. **Purgeable space**: what macOS frees when it needs room (downloaded iCloud files, snapshots, system caches), already counted as free; shown from 100 MB. **System and apps**: used space on the startup disk minus your measured home folder (macOS, apps, other users, local snapshots); without Full Disk Access it also holds the skipped folders, with a note; **View in Space Lens** opens the startup disk there to see where it goes. **Largest folders in your home**: the 12 biggest folders directly in your home, hidden ones and ~/Library included, measured in the background like Space Lens (without Full Disk Access, Desktop, Documents, Downloads, Movies, Music and Pictures are left out, with a note; a folder macOS won't let CleanMate read is listed under **Not readable** with **Open Settings…**, one whose size couldn't be measured under **Not measured**); while measuring, a progress bar shows the folder being measured and how much is measured so far, with **Stop** (also ⌘.), which ends it at once and keeps the sizes shown; after a clean or a move to the Trash in any module, only the folders holding what was removed (or put back) are measured again the next time the Overview shows, the others keep their size (when where is not known, e.g. a tool's own clean command, all are measured again); click one to open it in Space Lens, which then shows it without measuring again. The sizes of the last measure (folder names and sizes only) are kept in `~/Library/Application Support/CleanMate/home-folders.json`, so from the second launch the list and **System and apps** show at once, marked **Measured** with the date and time, while the folders are measured again; the new sizes then replace them (rows move into their new order, without motion when **Reduce motion** is on). A missing or damaged file only means waiting for the measure, as on the first launch. **System protection**: whether Gatekeeper, the firewall, System Integrity Protection and FileVault are on (green), off (orange) or could not be read (grey, **Unknown**), and the XProtect version with the date it was installed. They are read in the background with the status commands macOS ships (`spctl --status`, `socketfilterfw --getglobalstate`, `csrutil status`, `fdesetup status`; no administrator password, no network, a few seconds at most each) and from XProtect's Info.plist. When one is off, a sentence says what that means and **Open System Settings…** opens the pane where it is turned on (SIP is changed only from macOS Recovery). CleanMate shows these settings only; it never changes them and gives no score. **Refresh** (or ⌘R) reads everything again; after a cleanup the folders are measured again the next time the Overview is shown, and the protections are read again each time it is shown. |
| **Smart Scan** | System junk, developer junk, AI tool data and app caches in one scan — see [Smart Scan in detail](#smart-scan-in-detail). Items (simulator data too) are measured in parallel, so a scan finishes sooner. |
| **Privacy** | Per browser (Safari, Chrome, Edge, Brave, Arc, Opera, Vivaldi, Firefox, and the Beta/Dev/Canary channels of Chrome and Edge and Brave Beta/Nightly, each its own group shown only when installed and skipped while that channel runs): browsing history, download history, cookies, autofill form data (Firefox history and downloads are erased inside places.sqlite; bookmarks are kept; Safari history is skipped while Safari or a macOS service still has its database open). System: recent items (the Apple menu's recent applications, documents and servers, and each app's Open Recent list, one row each so you can keep some; an open app keeps its list, and the Finder sidebar lists — favorites, iCloud, projects — are never touched), download history (Gatekeeper's record of the name and web address of every file you downloaded, which macOS never prunes: only its records are erased, the files stay; skipped while the database is in use), QuickLook thumbnails, shell / REPL / less / Vim history, saved Terminal and iTerm2 session contents (skipped while the app runs; iTerm2 hidden when not installed or empty). Nothing is ticked by default. |
| **Large & Old Files** | Files ≥ 50 MB in your home folder — or only in the **Scope** you pick in the toolbar: Downloads, Desktop, Documents, each mounted external drive (listed by name), any folder inside your home or on an external drive via **Choose Folder…** (other folders outside home, folders in ~/Library and hidden folders are refused), or one of the last 5 chosen; on an external drive, hidden folders (its .Trashes, Spotlight index) and other drives mounted inside are skipped, and files go to that drive's own Trash; the scope is remembered between launches (a folder that is gone or no longer allowed falls back to your home folder); changing it scans again; a folder scope can also go down to **At least 1 MB** or **10 MB** (choosing a smaller size scans again, a larger one only filters; the home folder stays at 50 MB or more), and without Full Disk Access a folder macOS protects shows a request for access instead of being scanned — filtered by size, last use (**Over 1 month ago** to **Over 2 years ago**, or **Custom…** for any number of days; Spotlight's last-opened date, else last access; a later modification also counts) and kind — videos, audio, images, documents (PDF, Office, iWork, text, ebooks), code & projects (source, scripts, data files, and any file of no known kind inside a project folder: a Git repository or a folder with `package.json`, `Cargo.toml`, `Package.swift`…, below your home folder or a drive's root), archives, disk images, installers, other — each with its icon in the **Kind** menu and column, a toolbar search by file name or folder, a **Duplicates Only** filter (files with an identical copy, grouped together with a Copies column; only files of the same byte size are read and compared by SHA-256 (files whose beginning already differs are not read in full, and files are read a piece at a time, so it uses little memory even on very large files; the progress bar always reaches 100%, even if some files can't be read; hashes are remembered between launches, even when you stop the search midway, so unchanged files are not read again), hard links to one file count once; APFS clones (Finder's Duplicate, `cp -c`) are listed with their group, labelled **APFS clone — moving it frees no space** (or **frees only** the part they no longer share), and the selected size counts only what they free, so a group of clones shows 0 bytes; nothing is ticked for you; **Select Extra Copies** ticks every copy except one per group and except APFS clones: the oldest by modification date, ties go to the shortest path, counting copies hidden by other filters too; if every copy of a file is ticked, the Trash confirmation warns that none will be left), a **Similar Photos** mode in the same menu (All Files / Duplicates Only / Similar Photos) that groups photos which look alike — bursts, edits, resized or recompressed copies — in the same scope: JPEG, HEIC, PNG, TIFF and WebP files of at least 20 KB are compared on this Mac with Apple's Vision image feature prints (nothing is uploaded) and two photos count as similar when their prints are within a fixed distance, every photo of a group close to every other; each group shows thumbnails, size, dimensions and date, its photo with the most pixels (then the largest, then the newest file) marked **Suggested to keep**; **Sort By** lists the groups by **Space to Free** (default), **Date** (newest photo first) or **Number of Photos**; a photo opens with **Open** (right-click, or Return on the selected row), and the eye button or Space shows it in Quick Look; nothing is ticked for you, **Select All but Suggested** ticks the rest, and the Trash confirmation warns when every photo of a group is ticked; when tens of thousands of photos look nearly alike, it stops comparing after 2 million close pairs and says **Results may be incomplete** instead of leaving photos out silently; the search can be stopped (at once, also while photos are being analyzed), and prints are remembered between launches by path, size and modification date, so unchanged photos are not read again; it never reads inside a Photos library (`.photoslibrary`), an app or iCloud Drive, and follows the same Full Disk Access rules as the scan (no per-folder access dialog); moved photos are recorded, so `cleanmate restore --list` lists them under `Similar Photos` and `cleanmate restore` puts them back; `cleanmate similar` lists the same groups from the command line; a sortable Location column (containing folder, `~` for home), **Open** with the file's default app (double-click, Return or right-click, like Finder; more than 10 selected files are revealed in Finder instead), Quick Look with the eye button on each row or Space, **Reveal in Finder** in the right-click menu, **Select All**/**Deselect All** for the files the filters show, **Show in Space Lens** in the right-click menu. A file that has other hard links is marked **Hard link — moving it frees no space**. Selected files go to the Trash (⌘⌫ or the button, after a confirmation); the list stays, without the files moved, and nothing is scanned again: a bar above it says how much was moved, lists what couldn't be, and offers **Undo** (also ⌘Z, which lists the files put back again) and **View Moved Items…** until the next scan or until closed. **iCloud Drive**: with Full Disk Access, a home folder scan also lists the downloaded files of iCloud Drive (its own folder and each app's Documents folder in it, never an app's private data); files kept only in iCloud take no space and are not listed, and the scan never downloads anything. These rows and files of Desktop & Documents synced to iCloud show **iCloud** under the name; when every change is in iCloud, **Remove Download** (the link, or the right-click menu, after a confirmation) removes the copy on this Mac with macOS's own iCloud call: the file stays in iCloud Drive and Finder, and downloads again when opened. A file not uploaded yet, uploading or downloading, or with conflicting versions shows why and can't lose its download; the state is read again right before. Files inside iCloud Drive's own folder never go to the Trash from here (no checkbox); synced Desktop & Documents files can do either. The space freed is the file's size on disk just before; it is logged and the rows leave the list. Without Full Disk Access iCloud Drive is not read at all, so no access dialog appears. Folders are read in parallel, so a scan takes about half as long. |
| **Uninstaller** | Apps in /Applications and ~/Applications (including up to three subfolders down, e.g. `Adobe Photoshop 2025/`; never helper apps inside another app) with their data in ~/Library (Application Support, Caches, Preferences, Containers…), plus leftovers of apps already removed — also folders in `~/.config`, `~/.local/share` and `~/.local/state` that no installed app, command-line tool (in your PATH, the `PATH` set in your shell profile, or the usual bin folders) or Homebrew formula/cask is named after (marked "No app with this name is installed", never ticked, to the Trash; a CLI's own folders such as `claude`, `codex`, `gemini` and shared ones such as `fonts` are never listed). Data is matched by bundle id, a group container named `<TEAMID>.<group>` also by the team that signed the app (read from the selected app's code signature; listed as **Possibly related** instead when another installed app of the same vendor, or signed by the same team, may share it — telling that reads the other installed apps' signatures once per session, about a second for 120 apps, only when such a container is found; never a container of another release channel of the app or of another installed app's bundle id), and in Application Support, Caches and Logs also by a folder named like the app — its name, its bundle name or executable (`Code` for Visual Studio Code), or that name without spaces or with `-`/`_` (`DiskDrill`, `beekeeper-studio`) — unless another installed app goes by that name too. A folder in Application Support that only starts with the app's first word (`BraveSoftware` for Brave Browser, when no other app starts that way) is listed as **Possibly related**, and the app's config outside ~/Library — `~/.config/<name>`, `~/.local/share/<name>`, `~/.local/state/<name>`, `~/.<name>` or `~/.<name>-…` (`~/.config/zed`, `~/.local/state/zed`, `~/.cursor`) — is listed too; neither is ticked for you. Config is never shell or login files, keys (`.ssh`, `.gnupg`, `.aws`, `.kube`), `~/.config`, `~/.cache`, `~/.local`, `~/.local/share` or `~/.local/state` themselves (nor anything through a symlinked one), stores shared by developer tools (`.npm`, `.cargo`, `.android`…), a command-line tool's own config (`~/.claude`, `~/.gemini`…), nor a name used by a command-line tool installed outside the app. Each app row shows the app's size and, once measured in the background (all apps at once, so it fills in quickly), the size of its data ("45 MB · data 1.2 GB", the same files its detail lists, unticked ones included, except group containers matched only by the app's team; without Full Disk Access Containers and Group Containers are not counted). Sort the list by Name, Size (largest first, app + data once measured) or Last Used (oldest first) from the toolbar, and show only apps not used for over 3 months, 6 months or a year, or only apps from one source (it combines with the search); these choices are remembered. Each row shows where the app came from: **App Store** (it holds the App Store's receipt), **Homebrew cask (<token>)**, **Setapp** (in a `Setapp` folder of Applications) or **Downloaded** (anything else). When macOS has no last-opened date for an app, an open app counts as used today; otherwise the row shows an estimate ("Last used ≈ 3 days ago") from when its preferences, cache or Application Support folder last changed, or **Unknown** when there is none. Unknown apps sort last and are left out of the "not used for" filter; a note below the list says how many and **Show** lists them too. Drag an app onto the window to select it. Tick the checkbox of several apps (the **Applications** header ticks every app listed, for example after **Not Used for a Year**) to uninstall them together: the panel lists the ticked apps, each with its size (the app plus its data surely its own) and **Show Files** to review it alone, and **Uninstall N Apps…** asks once, naming them. Each app then goes to the Trash with its ticked data only — **Possibly related** folders and config are never part of a batch; review an app on its own to remove those. An open app is skipped (not counted in the size; the panel and the confirmation name it), and an app locked in Finder or one that can't go to the Trash is left with its reason in the result; neither stops the others. One Undo puts back everything moved. Ticks stay while you search, filter or look at one app. An app installed with `brew install --cask` (found in Homebrew's Caskroom, without running brew) shows **Homebrew cask (<token>)**; uninstalling it moves the app to the Trash like any app, then runs `brew uninstall --cask <token>` (no `--zap`, no `--force`, no auto-update) so Homebrew drops its record and `brew upgrade` doesn't put the app back. If brew fails or isn't installed, the app stays in the Trash and the result says to run `brew uninstall --cask <token>` in Terminal; the attempt is recorded in **Settings › Activity** either way. While a `brew` command runs, a cask app is not uninstalled (try again when it finishes). Undo puts back only the app; Homebrew no longer lists it (`brew install --cask --adopt <token>` takes it back). **Reset…** (next to **Uninstall…**) fixes a misbehaving app without reinstalling it: a sheet lists the app's own data in ~/Library (caches, preferences, Application Support, containers and group containers, saved state, logs, WebKit and HTTP storage) with sizes, all ticked; untick any to keep it. Only those go to the Trash; the app itself, its launch agents and login items, "Possibly related" folders and config outside ~/Library are never part of a reset. While the app is open it becomes **Quit and Reset**: CleanMate asks the app to quit and waits up to 10 seconds like **Quit and Uninstall…**, then moves the ticked data; if it doesn't quit, nothing is moved. When the selected app is open, **Uninstall…** becomes **Quit and Uninstall…**: CleanMate asks the app to quit (never forces it) and waits up to 10 seconds (**Stop** ends the wait and moves nothing), then uninstalls it in the same step; if it doesn't quit (for example it asks to save a document), the result says so and nothing is moved. Unticking the app's own row does the same from the detail list. Right-click a data row and choose **Show in Space Lens** to see it in its folder. Everything goes to the Trash. An app owned by the system or another user goes to the Trash the way Finder does it: macOS asks for an administrator password; if you cancel, nothing is moved and its data stays (putting such an app back with Undo may not be permitted — it then stays in the Trash). An App Store app macOS won't let CleanMate move never brings up that password prompt: the result says to remove it in Launchpad or drag it to the Trash in Finder, with **Reveal in Finder**, and nothing is moved; its data then shows under **Leftovers of Removed Apps** (or Reset it while the app is still installed). In a batch it is skipped with that reason. **Unknown Hidden Config** lists hidden items in your home folder and items in `~/.config` that no known tool, installed command-line tool or app explains, flagging ones that contain a program/script or changed in the last 7 days — warning only, CleanMate never removes them (Reveal in Finder / Quick Look). **Extensions** lists preference panes, Quick Look plug-ins, services, screen savers, input methods, internet plug-ins and Spotlight importers in `~/Library` (not `/Library`) with their type, size, date and owning app (same bundle id or vendor), marking ones whose app is gone as **App removed** (apps open from outside the Applications folders count as installed). Nothing is ticked for you: tick items and choose **Move to Trash…**; only an item directly in one of those folders, with the right type (`.prefPane`, `.qlgenerator`, `.workflow`, `.saver`, `.app`/`.bundle` input method, `.plugin`/`.webplugin`, `.mdimporter`), not a symlink and not Apple's is moved (checked again right before), and Undo / ⌘Z puts it back. While System Settings is open, preference panes can't be ticked (**Close System Settings to move**, with a **Quit System Settings** button). |
| **Space Lens** | Browse any folder (**Choose Folder…**; the **Location** menu lists the last 5 chosen under **Recent**) sorted by size (measured like `du`; a folder macOS won't let CleanMate read shows **Not Readable** instead of a size, and opening it says so with **Open Settings…** rather than showing an empty folder; one whose size couldn't be measured shows **Not measured**) with a donut chart of its contents (a stacked bar on macOS 13), drill down (breadcrumb from your home folder for folders inside it, ⌘[ to go back), search the current folder by name (the total and donut still cover the whole folder), Quick Look with Space, move items in your home folder to the Trash (⌘⌫ or the button, after a confirmation). The **Location** menu also lists each mounted drive by name: a folder on an external drive opens with the breadcrumb from the drive and is measured on that drive only; on a writable external drive, files and folders go to that drive's own Trash (a folder only when it holds no hidden items besides Finder's `.DS_Store` and `._` files, no packages, links or other drives; hidden items, its .Trashes and the drive itself are never moved), and Undo puts them back. Read-only, network and Time Machine drives are view-only. **Startup Disk** in the **Location** menu lists the top level of the startup disk (Applications, Library, System, Users…) with sizes, measured like `du -x`: another volume mounted inside (the data volume in /System/Volumes, a drive in /Volumes) is never counted again; it shows a drive icon and an external drive opens as its own location. Any folder outside your home folder and off external drives opens with the breadcrumb from the startup disk; only the folder shown is measured, never its parents. Everything there is view-only, with a lock whose tooltip says why: part of macOS protected by System Integrity Protection, the system library (Smart Scan cleans what is safe in it), another user's folder, apps (remove them with the Uninstaller), the folder holding your home folder, or simply outside your home folder; items of your home folder reached from there move to the Trash as usual. Without Full Disk Access the protected folders of your home are skipped there too, so no macOS access dialog appears. **Show Hidden Files** in the toolbar (⇧⌘., on by default, remembered) hides dotfiles and items Finder hides when off; they still count in the total and the donut's **Other**. Big folders are measured in parallel, so a scan of your home folder takes about half as long. |
| **Login Items** | Your launch agents with owner app and state: disable, enable or remove (plist to the Trash). After a removal the bar offers **Undo** (also ⌘Z), which puts the plist back and loads the agent again, and **View Moved Items…**; later, `cleanmate restore --list` lists it and `cleanmate restore` puts it back (then log in again). When an action fails, the alert gives the real reason: what `launchctl` printed with its exit code, or the file system's (not permitted, locked, in use), plus **Open Settings…** when Full Disk Access would help. The plist's Label is read again right before; if it changed, can't be read or another plist in the folder uses the same Label, CleanMate refuses instead of touching another service. System agents are read-only. |
| **Maintenance** | Five fixes macOS users run often, plus a Homebrew update, each with a **Run** button (**Stop** while it runs: the running tool is ended at once, what it already did stays done and the steps after it don’t run), its status (running, done, failed or stopped, with the time) and the tools' output under **Output**. **Rebuild Launch Services** removes duplicate and outdated apps from **Open With** menus: it compacts the Launch Services database (`lsregister -gc`) and registers the apps of every domain again (`lsregister -r -f -apps local,system,user`; the old `-kill` option no longer exists). **Reset Quick Look** restarts Quick Look and clears its thumbnail cache (`qlmanage -r`, `qlmanage -r cache`) when previews are blank, wrong or slow. **Restart Finder and Dock** (`killall Finder`, `killall Dock`; macOS starts both again) asks first, because Finder windows close and a copy in progress in Finder stops. **Reset Font Cache** fixes text drawn in the wrong font or as boxes: it removes your own font registry (`atsutil databases -removeUser`; the system one needs root) and restarts the font server (`atsutil server -shutdown`, `-ping`; on macOS 14 and later these do nothing); open apps may need a restart. **Clear Icon Cache** fixes wrong or blank icons in the Dock and Finder: it deletes `com.apple.dock.iconcache`, `com.apple.iconservices` and `com.apple.iconservices.store` in your user cache folder (`getconf DARWIN_USER_CACHE_DIR`; never `/Library/Caches`, which needs root), then restarts the Dock (`killall Dock`), after asking first. **Update and Clean Homebrew** (listed only when Homebrew is installed) runs `brew update`, `brew upgrade --formula` and `brew cleanup --prune=all`, in that order: formulae only, never casks, since a cask upgrade may ask for a password or replace an app that is open. brew gets no input (a step that would ask anything fails instead of waiting), no auto-update after the first step and no hints; the three steps together are stopped after 15 minutes. brew's output shows live while it runs, and a last line says how many formulae were upgraded and how much space the cleanup reported freeing. An error from brew shows in the output and stops the steps after it. While another `brew` command runs (in Terminal, for example), the task is locked with a note until it finishes. One task runs at a time, steps run in order and the first failing step stops the task; each tool is stopped after 60 seconds (Homebrew: see above). None needs an administrator password, none removes your files, and each run adds a line to the activity log (**Ran** or **Failed**, no size). Tasks that need root (`purge`, `mdutil`, `dscacheutil`) are left out on purpose. **Time Machine local snapshots** lists the snapshots of the startup disk by date (`tmutil listlocalsnapshots /`, read each time Maintenance is shown), each with **Delete…**, plus **Delete All…** when there are several. Both ask first: a local snapshot is a backup copy Time Machine keeps on the Mac, and deleting it can't be undone; backups on the Time Machine disk are not affected. Each snapshot is deleted with `tmutil deletelocalsnapshots <date>` (no administrator password; stopped after 120 seconds), one after another; one that fails doesn't stop the others, and the list is read again afterwards. Each deletion adds a **Ran** or **Failed** line with the snapshot's date to the activity log; their size can't be measured without administrator rights, so the freed space is not counted. While snapshots are being deleted no task runs, and the other way round. |

## Settings, shortcuts and Undo

**Settings** (⌘,): optional menu bar item (off by default) showing free space out of the disk's size (as in Finder),
the last Smart Scan ("Junk found: 2.4 GB (scanned …)": everything the scan found, ticked or not, and what cleaning
it freed; click it to open Smart Scan) and the space freed in the last 30 days, with **Scan Now** (opens Smart Scan and starts a scan; disabled, like ⌘R, while a confirmation is open or
something is being cleaned), **Open CleanMate** and **Quit**; **In the background** (both off by default, and only
with the menu bar item on): **Open at login** adds CleanMate to System Settings › General › Login Items, so it starts in
the menu bar when you log in, with no window, no scan and no network use (the update check you chose waits until you open the window); if macOS
has not allowed it yet, **Allow…** opens Login Items. Turning the menu bar item off also turns Open at login off.
**Menu bar only** takes CleanMate out of the Dock and ⌘Tab while its window is closed; **Open CleanMate** (or opening
the app again in Finder) brings the window and the Dock icon back, and **Quit** in the menu bar quits it. The welcome
guide's last step offers both (turning either on there also shows the menu bar item); Full Disk Access status, age thresholds (logs, temporary files, AI sessions, installers in Downloads — default 30 days, files in Downloads — default 90 days, both 7 to 365), remembered
choices, low disk warning, scan reminder, updates (see [Privacy](#privacy)), excluded folders, and the activity log.
**Remind me to scan** (Settings › General, Off by default): Weekly or Monthly posts a local notification at 10:00
about a week or a month after you last opened CleanMate or finished a Smart Scan (each launch, except a start at login,
and each finished Smart Scan moves it), then keeps repeating while CleanMate goes unused. Clicking it opens Smart Scan without starting
a scan; it is not shown while CleanMate is the app in front. macOS asks for permission the first time you turn it on;
if notifications are off, **Turn On Notifications…** opens System Settings › Notifications. Monthly reminders use
the 28th at the latest, so no month is skipped.
**Freed 1.2 GB**: when a Smart Scan clean or a Privacy erase finishes while CleanMate is not the app in front, a
local notification says how much it freed (or that it finished, when nothing had a size); clicking it opens that
module. It is posted only when notifications are already allowed (by the scan reminder or the low disk warning): it
never asks for permission and never starts a clean.
**Warn when free space is below** (Settings › General, 10% by default; Off, 5%, 15% or 20%): a local notification
such as "“Macintosh HD” has 8 GB free (8%)" when the disk holding your home folder (the one the menu bar item shows)
has less free space than that share of its size. It is checked when CleanMate opens, when Smart Scan finishes a scan or

## Smart Scan in detail

Each row in Smart Scan, Privacy and Review Before Cleaning has an ⓘ button (**Why is this safe?**, reached with Tab and opened with Space or a click). Its popover says what the item is, where it is (the folders scanned), what cleaning does (deleted and made again by the app or tool, moved to the Trash, erased for good, or the tool's own clean command; whether it is ticked by default) and when it is skipped (app open, tool running, files in use, recent changes, folders always kept, Exclusions), with **Open Exclusions…**. Everything but the first part is read from the item's own rules in the catalog (`CleanMate/Engine/*Catalog.swift` and the item files next to them, through `ItemExplanation`), so it always matches what the scan and the clean do; the catalog is the source of truth, and this page describes it.

**System**
- User caches (a folder named after a running app's bundle ID or name is skipped; generic vendor names such as Google or Microsoft don't count; so is a folder holding a file another process has open, e.g. a CLI daemon; Poetry's project virtualenvs in `~/Library/Caches/pypoetry/virtualenvs` are never removed; updates apps downloaded with Sparkle are listed under **Downloaded app updates** instead).
- Caches of sandboxed (App Store) apps inside their containers and of app groups (Group Containers, e.g. Microsoft Office, Slack) — only with Full Disk Access; never Apple's or running apps. An app group named `<TEAMID>.<group>` is kept while any app signed by that team runs (the team is read from the running apps' code signatures, nothing else); a `group.<id>` group, or one while a running app's signature can't be read, is kept while an app of the same vendor runs.
- Copies of attachments opened in Mail — only with Full Disk Access and Mail closed; older than the old-logs age, unticked, moved to the Trash.
- iPhone and iPad backups made by Finder — only with Full Disk Access; unticked, moved to the Trash; each backup listed with its device name and date when expanded, with its own checkbox so you can keep some; skipped while a backup runs or if written to in the last hour.
- Downloaded iOS/iPadOS updates (`.ipsw` in `~/Library/iTunes/iPhone|iPad|iPod Software Updates`, plus Apple Configurator's firmware cache with Full Disk Access) — only `.ipsw` files, each listed with device and version (e.g. "iPhone · 18.1 (22B83)") and its own checkbox; unticked, moved to the Trash; skipped while Finder or Apple Configurator updates a device.
- **macOS installers** — `Install macOS *.app` and `Install OS X *.app` directly in `/Applications` or `~/Applications` (12–15 GB each, left behind after an upgrade or after making a USB installer). Only Apple's real installer apps (bundle ID `com.apple.InstallAssistant…`), never through a symlink. Each one is listed with its macOS version (e.g. "macOS Sonoma 14.6" for Big Sur and later; older installers from `InstallInfo.plist`) and size, with its own checkbox; unticked (you may keep one to install again), moved to the Trash so Undo puts it back. Installers owned by root or in a folder you cannot write to are listed as locked rows with the reason "Administrator privileges required — delete with Finder" and never prompt for a password. Skipped while an installer runs (the installer app, `startosinstall`, `createinstallmedia`). No Full Disk Access needed. `/Library/Updates`, `/System` and installers in Downloads (**Old installers**) are never touched.
- **Old installers** — only with Full Disk Access: disk images, packages, ISO images, Xcode archives (`.dmg`, `.pkg`, `.iso`, `.xip`) and zip files with an app at their top level (`Name.app/`), directly in `~/Downloads` (never in subfolders, never through a symlink) and older than the installers age (Settings › General, default 30 days). Each file is listed by name and size with its own checkbox; unticked, moved to the Trash. Skipped: a file that is open (such as a mounted disk image) and a download still in progress (`.download`, `.crdownload`, `.part`, `.aria2`, `.!qB` next to it).
- **Old downloads** — only with Full Disk Access: files and folders directly in `~/Downloads` that were not modified, created, added to Downloads or opened (read, or Finder's "Last opened") for the downloads age (Settings › General, default 90 days); a folder only when nothing inside was modified, created or read either. Each one is listed by name, the date it was last touched and size, with its own checkbox; unticked, moved to the Trash, and every date is checked again right before the move. Skipped: hidden files (`.DS_Store`, `.localized`), symlinks, installers (listed under Old installers), a file that is open or a folder holding one, a download still in progress, and a folder with more than 10,000 items, an unreadable folder or a socket inside. Nothing is listed when `~/Downloads` is a symlink.
- **Old screenshots** — only with Full Disk Access: screenshots and screen recordings directly in the folder macOS saves them to (Desktop, or the folder chosen in the Screenshot app's Options › Save to, when it is inside your home folder and not a symlink) that were not modified, created, added or opened for the downloads age (Settings › General, default 90 days). A file counts as a screenshot when the Screenshot app marked it (`kMDItemIsScreenCapture`, read from the file itself) or when its name starts with "Screenshot", "Screen Shot", "Screen Recording", "CleanShot" or the Vietnamese "Ảnh màn hình", "Ảnh chụp màn hình", "Ghi màn hình", "Bản ghi màn hình" and ends in `.png`, `.jpg`, `.jpeg`, `.heic`, `.mov` or `.mp4`. Each one is listed by name, date and size with its own checkbox; unticked, moved to the Trash (Undo puts it back), dates checked again right before the move. Skipped: hidden files, symlinks, files in Exclusions and files that are open. When screenshots are saved to Downloads they are listed here, not under Old downloads (which then also keeps the Downloads folder holding the save folder).
- **Abandoned downloads** — only with Full Disk Access: unfinished downloads directly in `~/Downloads` — Chrome, Edge and Brave `.crdownload`, Firefox `.part`, Safari `.download` (a folder), aria2 `.aria2` and qBittorrent `.!qB`, any case — that were not modified, created, added or opened for 7 days. Firefox's empty file of the real name next to its `.part` and the file aria2 downloads into next to its `.aria2` go with it: same row, same checkbox, both moved to the Trash (Undo puts both back), and when that file was used in the last 7 days the download is kept. Each one is listed by name, date and size with its own checkbox; unticked, dates checked again right before the move. Skipped: a download whose file (or its companion) is open — still downloading —, hidden files, symlinks and files in Exclusions (an excluded companion keeps its download too). Old installers and Old downloads never list these.
- Old logs, crash reports, old temporary files (an item holding a file a process has open is never old), the Trash (unticked by default).
- **Trash on external drives** — only with Full Disk Access: what you deleted from a drive stays in that drive's own Trash (`.Trashes/<your user id>`), which the home Trash item does not count. Each mounted external drive whose Trash holds something is listed by name with its size and its own checkbox; unticked by default, emptied permanently (the Trash folder itself stays). Never other users' Trash folders, read-only, network or Time Machine drives, a Trash that is (or sits behind) a symlink, or a drive whose Trash holds a file still open (Finder still moving files there).

**Developer junk** (each item hidden when empty)
- **Xcode**: DerivedData (skipped while Xcode or `xcodebuild` runs; each project can be ticked on its own when expanded, shown with its workspace path and last use; shared caches such as ModuleCache are marked "shared by all projects"), device support (each OS version can be ticked on its own, e.g. "iOS 18.1 (22B83)"), old archives (moved to the Trash; each archive can be ticked on its own when expanded), old Xcode versions (see below), simulator data (unticked; one row per available simulator with its name, OS and last use, erased with `simctl erase` so the simulator stays, empty as new; a running simulator is locked; never deletes a simulator), test device clones (unticked; the copies Xcode makes in `~/Library/Developer/XCTestDevices` to run tests in parallel, one row each, made again by the next parallel test run), both skipped while Xcode, Simulator or `xcodebuild` runs, SwiftUI Previews simulators, simulator caches (skipped while Xcode or Simulator runs), old simulator runtimes (unticked; only a runtime that a newer installed runtime of the same platform replaces and that was not used for 30 days, removed with `simctl runtime delete`; skipped while Xcode or Simulator runs).
- **Package caches**: SwiftPM, CocoaPods, npm, npx (skipped while `npx` runs), nvm downloads (`~/.nvm/.cache`, never installed versions), Yarn (the Yarn Berry global cache `~/.yarn/berry/cache` is a separate unticked item: Plug'n'Play projects need `yarn install` again), pnpm, Bun, pip, uv, Go, Cargo (Cargo, Yarn, Bun and pip caches are skipped while that tool runs), rustup downloads (`~/.rustup/downloads` and `tmp`, not toolchains: see below; skipped while `rustup` runs, not during a `cargo` build; a download still open is kept), Gradle (caches, each folder's size kept between scans like stale projects' below and measured again when anything up to two levels inside it changed; downloaded wrapper versions unticked), Maven, Homebrew, Composer. Unticked, deleted: **CocoaPods spec repo clones** (each git clone in `~/.cocoapods/repos`, such as the old `master`, one row each; never the `trunk` CDN index; skipped while `pod` runs), **Xcode documentation cache** (`~/Library/Developer/Xcode/DocumentationCache`) and **Xcode device logs** (`iOS`, `watchOS` and `tvOS Device Logs`), both skipped while Xcode runs. Two unticked items: **conda package archives** (`*.tar.bz2`, `*.conda` and the index `cache/` in the `pkgs` folders of Miniconda, Anaconda, Miniforge, Mambaforge, micromamba, `~/.conda` and the `pkgs_dirs` of `~/.condarc`; never unpacked packages or `envs/`; skipped while conda, mamba or micromamba runs) and the **Dart and Flutter pub cache** (`~/.pub-cache/hosted` and `git/cache`, or `PUB_CACHE` from your shell profile; never `credentials.json` or `bin/`; skipped while dart or flutter runs; `pub get` downloads them again).
- **Build tool caches**, each its own unticked item, deleted, skipped while the tool runs, a file in use kept: **Deno** (`~/Library/Caches/deno` or `DENO_DIR`; never `location_data`, scripts' local storage), **Zig** (`~/.cache/zig` or `ZIG_GLOBAL_CACHE_DIR`), **Bazel** (only `cache` and `install` in `~/.cache/bazel/_bazel_<user>`, never a workspace's output base; Bazel's macOS default `/private/var/tmp` is outside your home and not scanned), **ccache** (`~/Library/Caches/ccache`, `~/.ccache` or `CCACHE_DIR`; never `ccache.conf`), **sccache** (`~/Library/Caches/Mozilla.sccache` or `SCCACHE_DIR`), **NuGet** (`v3-cache` and `http-cache`; never `~/.nuget/packages`), **Poetry** (`cache` and `artifacts` in `~/Library/Caches/pypoetry` or `POETRY_CACHE_DIR`; never `virtualenvs`), **pre-commit** (`~/.cache/pre-commit` or `PRE_COMMIT_HOME`; hooks install again on the next commit) and **pipx run** (`~/Library/Caches/pipx`, `~/.local/pipx/.cache` or `$PIPX_HOME/.cache`; never apps installed with `pipx install`). The next build or run makes them again, slower once.
- **Stale node_modules**: the `node_modules` folder of each JavaScript project (a folder with `package.json` and `node_modules`) in `~/Developer`, `~/Projects`, `~/Code`, `~/src`, and with Full Disk Access `~/Documents` and `~/Desktop`, up to 4 folders down. A project is stale when its `package.json`, lockfiles (`package-lock.json`, `npm-shrinkwrap.json`, `yarn.lock`, `pnpm-lock.yaml`, `bun.lock`, `bun.lockb`), Git index and `node_modules` folder were not modified for 90 days — nor those of the folders above it (a package of a monorepo, a project inside an active repository). Each project is listed by name with its last change and its own checkbox; unticked, moved to the Trash (Undo puts it back; `npm install` downloads it again). Skipped: a project where node, Bun, pnpm, npm, Yarn or Deno runs (or that one of them was started with), a project holding an open file, an excluded one. Never a `node_modules` inside another one, in a hidden folder, in ~/Library, in an app bundle, or reached through a symlink. The size is what moving the folder frees: pnpm and Bun share files with their store as hard links or APFS clones, so a file hard-linked to a file outside the folder counts nothing, a file linked twice inside it counts once, and a clone counts only the bytes it doesn't share (nothing for an untouched clone). Two clones of one file both inside the folder count nothing either, so the size can be a little less than what is freed, never more. Reading this costs one extra lookup per file, so the first scan of a large project takes longer (about 3× on 1.2 million files); sizes are then remembered until the folder changes.
- **Stale build folders**: in the same project folders, found by the same walk, each build folder only next to the file that makes it, at the project's top: Cargo's `target` (`Cargo.toml`) and Maven's (`pom.xml`); Gradle and Android `build` and `.gradle` (`build.gradle(.kts)` or `settings.gradle(.kts)`) and a module's `build` (`app/build`) when that module has its own `build.gradle(.kts)`; SwiftPM `.build` (`Package.swift`); Flutter and Dart `.dart_tool` and `build` (`pubspec.yaml`); with a `package.json`, `.next` (`next.config.*`), `.nuxt` and `.output` (`nuxt.config.*`), `.svelte-kit` (`svelte.config.*`), `.angular/cache` (`angular.json`) and `.turbo` (`turbo.json`); Haskell `.stack-work` (`stack.yaml`) and `dist-newstyle` (`cabal.project` or a `*.cabal`); Elixir `_build` and `deps` (`mix.exs`); Zig `.zig-cache` and `zig-cache` (`build.zig`; never `zig-out`, what the project installs); a Python virtual environment (`.venv` or `venv` holding `pyvenv.cfg`, next to `pyproject.toml`, `setup.py` or a `requirements*.txt`); CocoaPods' `Pods` (`Podfile`). A folder named `build`, `dist` or `out` without such a file is never listed. A project is stale when its project files and lockfiles (those above, plus `Cargo.lock`, `setup.cfg`, `Pipfile(.lock)`, `poetry.lock`, `uv.lock`, `pdm.lock`, `Podfile.lock`, `gradle.properties`, `gradle/libs.versions.toml`, `Package.resolved`, `pubspec.lock`, `stack.yaml.lock`, `cabal.project.freeze`, `mix.lock`, `build.zig.zon` and JavaScript's lockfiles), Git index and the build folder itself were not modified for 90 days — nor those of the folders above it. Each folder is listed with its project's path and its path in the project (`~/Developer/api — target/`, `~/Developer/android — app/build/`) and last change, with its own checkbox; unticked, moved to the Trash (Undo puts it back; the project's build tool makes it again). Skipped: a project where one of its tools runs (cargo, Gradle or Maven (Java), Swift, Flutter (Dart), Node, Bun, Deno, Stack, Cabal, GHC, Elixir, Zig, Python, uv or CocoaPods (Ruby)), a project holding an open file, an excluded one. Never inside `node_modules`, another build folder, a hidden folder, ~/Library or an app bundle, or reached through a symlink; each folder is checked again right before it moves. Sizes count what moving the folder frees: a file hard-linked to one outside it counts nothing, and in a virtual environment (uv clones packages from its cache) a clone counts only the bytes it doesn't share.
- **Stale Terraform providers**: in the same project folders, found by the same walk, only `.terraform/providers` and `.terraform/modules` of a Terraform or OpenTofu project (next to a `*.tf` file). Stale when its `*.tf` files, `.terraform.lock.hcl`, `terraform.tfstate`, Git index and the folder itself were not modified for 90 days, nor those of the folders above it. One row per folder (`~/Developer/infra — .terraform/providers/`), unticked, moved to the Trash; `terraform init` downloads them again. Never `.terraform/terraform.tfstate`, `.terraform/environment`, the project's state files or code. Skipped while `terraform`, `tofu` or a provider plugin runs in the project.
- Both items share one walk of the project folders (each folder read in parallel), and remember each folder's size in `~/Library/Caches/com.homielab.CleanMate/folder-sizes.json` (at most 1,000 folders): a folder is measured again only when its own modification date or subfolder count changed, so the next Smart Scan skips walking hundreds of thousands of untouched files. A change deep inside alone keeps the old size until then.
- **Unused Homebrew dependencies**: formulae installed only as dependencies of formulae since uninstalled, as `brew autoremove --dry-run` lists them (no casks). - **Final Cut Pro and iMovie render files**: the `Render Files` folders inside Final Cut Pro (`.fcpbundle`) and iMovie (`.imovielibrary`) libraries kept in Movies, Desktop or Documents or one folder down (needs Full Disk Access), often many GB. One row per library (its name, the app and when it last changed) with the total of its events' render files; nothing is ticked, so you can keep the renders of a project you are working on. Deleted: the app renders them again when a project is played or exported, so that first open is slower. Skipped while Final Cut Pro or iMovie runs, and a library with a file in use is kept. Libraries on other drives or in **Settings › Exclusions** are not scanned. Only `Render Files` is removed: the library, its media (`Original Media`, `Transcoded Media`), proxies, analysis files and databases are never touched.
- **Google Drive cache**, **Dropbox cache**, **OneDrive cache & logs**: what cloud sync apps keep locally besides your synced files, often 5–30 GB Finder doesn't show. Google Drive: each account's `content_cache` and the logs in `~/Library/Application Support/Google/DriveFS`, only parts nothing in changed for 7 days, so a file still waiting to upload is kept (files made available offline are downloaded again when opened). Dropbox: `.dropbox.cache` of each Dropbox folder named in `~/.dropbox/info.json` (temporary and recently deleted copies), also in `~/Library/CloudStorage/Dropbox*` with Full Disk Access, and `~/.dropbox/logs`. OneDrive (needs Full Disk Access): the updater's logs and the Caches of its container (not listed again under **Sandboxed app caches**). Unticked and deleted (the apps download again what they need); each is locked while its app, a helper or its Finder extension runs, and files in use are kept. Synced files, sign-ins, settings and the apps' databases (`DriveFS/<account>/*.db`, `metadata_sqlite_db`, `~/.dropbox/instance*`) are never touched. Still, let Google Drive finish uploading before quitting it: a file not uploaded yet may only exist in its cache.
- **Telegram media cache**, **Spotify stream cache**: media these apps keep outside `~/Library/Caches`, often 5–20 GB. Telegram for macOS (needs Full Disk Access): `postbox/media` and `cache` of each account in `~/Library/Group Containers/6N38VWS5BX.ru.keepcoder.Telegram/<channel>/account-*`; the database (`postbox/db`), keys and sign-in are never touched. Spotify: `~/Library/Application Support/Spotify/PersistentCache/Storage` (songs downloaded for offline use are there too and must be downloaded again). Unticked and deleted (the apps download again what they need); each is locked while its app runs, and files in use are kept.
- **Zalo**, **Canva**, **Microsoft Teams**, **WhatsApp**: caches of chat, meeting and design apps, often several GB. Zalo: the Chromium caches (`Cache`, `Code Cache`, `GPUCache`, Dawn/shader caches, `Service Worker/CacheStorage`) at the top of `~/Library/Application Support/ZaloData` and in its partitions, plus `~/Library/Caches/com.vng.zalo`; your messages, received photos and files (`ZaloData/media`) and the older `ZaloPC` folder are never touched. Canva: the same caches in `~/Library/Application Support/Canva` plus `~/Library/Caches/com.canva.CanvaDesktop`. The new Microsoft Teams (needs Full Disk Access): `Caches`, `Logs` and the web view's caches of each profile in its container `com.microsoft.teams2`. WhatsApp (needs Full Disk Access): the Caches of its container `net.whatsapp.WhatsApp`; chats and media live in its group container and are never touched. Cookies, local storage, IndexedDB, session storage and settings, and so your sign-in, always stay. Ticked and deleted (the apps make them again); each is hidden when the app is not installed and locked while it or a helper runs. Their folders are not counted again under **User caches** or **Sandboxed app caches**.
- **Steam**, **Epic Games Launcher**, **Battle.net**, **GOG Galaxy**: caches and logs game launchers keep in `~/Library/Application Support`, 0.5–3 GB with Steam. Steam: `htmlcache`, `config/htmlcache`, `appcache/httpcache`, `appcache/librarycache` (library artwork; custom artwork is kept), `depotcache`, `logs`, `dumps` and `steamapps/shadercache`. Epic: `Saved/webcache*` and `Saved/Logs` in `Epic/EpicGamesLauncher`. Battle.net: `BrowserCaches`, `Cache` and `Logs`. GOG Galaxy: `GOG.com/Galaxy/webcache`. Ticked and deleted, each folder emptied and kept; the launcher downloads or writes them again. Skipped while the launcher or one of its helpers runs. Installed games, downloads in progress (`steamapps/downloading`), `*.acf` and Epic install manifests, `userdata` (saves), settings and sign-ins are never touched. What the launchers keep in `~/Library/Caches` and `~/Library/Logs` stays with User caches and User logs, so nothing is counted twice.
- **Minecraft**, **Prism Launcher**: `~/Library/Application Support/minecraft` (`logs`, `crash-reports`, `webcache2`, `webcache`) and `PrismLauncher` (`logs`, `cache`, and `instances/*/.minecraft` or `minecraft` `logs` and `crash-reports`). Ticked and deleted, each folder emptied and kept; skipped while the launcher runs, and files the game has open are left. Worlds, mods, resource packs, shader packs, screenshots, settings, versions, accounts and instances stored outside the data folder are never touched.
- **Apple Podcasts: downloaded episodes**, **Apple Podcasts cache** (need Full Disk Access): Podcasts keeps downloaded episodes in `~/Library/Group Containers/243LU875E5.groups.com.apple.podcasts/Library/Cache`, often several GB Finder doesn't show. Episodes are the audio and video files directly in that folder, one row each; the cache is the rest of it (artwork, audio cached while streaming). Unticked and deleted: Podcasts downloads an episode again when you play it. Locked while Podcasts runs, and files in use are kept. The library database (`Documents/MTLibrary.sqlite`: subscriptions, played state), settings and downloads in progress (`Library/Caches`) are never touched.

**How it cleans**
- Ticking only some backups, archives, DerivedData projects, device support versions, models or snapshots makes the item's checkbox mixed and its size column shows "selected / total"; those per-entry ticks last until the next scan and every entry is listed (no cap). VoiceOver reads each entry as its label, date and size, not its long path.
- Official tool commands (`npm cache clean`, `brew cleanup`, `go clean`, `simctl`…) are used when installed.
- Sizes are what the command really frees (`brew cleanup --dry-run`, `docker system df`); pnpm has no dry run, and Bun and uv share files with your projects as APFS clones, so their sizes are shown as "Up to …" and kept out of the sure total; after cleaning, the result says how much of what was freed came from them (less space may really be freed). Homebrew's size is reused from the last scan while its cache, Cellar and Caskroom are unchanged (up to 6 hours).
- A file with several hard links in one item is counted once, so sizes match `du -sk`; an item with a subfolder CleanMate can't read says **Some items could not be read — the size may be larger**.
- Files and folders that are locked or protected by macOS (e.g. the empty `com.apple.*` folders in the temporary folder) are never listed: removing them could only fail.

## Safety

- **Every failure says why.** When a clean, a move to the Trash, an uninstall or a reset leaves something in place, the done screen says **N items couldn’t be cleaned** and **Show Details…** lists each one (its path, or the item for a tool's step) grouped by reason, each with what to do: not permitted (grant Full Disk Access, with **Open Settings…**), in use (quit the app), locked in Finder, the drive has no Trash or is read-only, an iCloud file not downloaded, changed since the scan, kept by the safety rules or your exclusions, or a tool (`brew`, `docker`, `tmutil`…) that exited with its code and the last line it printed. Each one is also a failed line with its reason in Settings › Activity.
- **Preview, then confirm.** Every module lists what it found with sizes; nothing is removed until you confirm.
- **Trash vs delete.** Regenerable data (caches, logs, developer junk) and privacy traces are deleted. User files,
  apps, app data, old AI sessions, old Xcode archives, Xcode and Unity Editor versions, stale `node_modules` and build folders, and launch agents are moved to the Trash, so you can put them back.
- **Your choices stick.** Items you untick stay unticked on the next scan (Settings › General › Remembered choices › **Reset…**, which asks before forgetting them).
- **Turn off whole groups** (Settings › Scan Groups): a group switched off there (for example Developer Junk or AI Tools) is not scanned nor shown, so Smart Scan finishes sooner; the results say how many groups are off, with an Open Settings button. With every group off, Scan is disabled. Privacy has no such switch (its few browser groups are quick to scan and nothing is ticked by default).
- **Hard blocklist**, checked when scanning and again right before every removal: only paths inside your home or
  temporary folder (plus macOS installer apps in `/Applications`, only moved to the Trash, what you pick on an external drive in Large & Old Files and Space Lens, only moved to that drive's Trash, and your own Trash folder on external drives, which Smart Scan only empties); never `.ssh`, `.gnupg`, `.aws`, `.kube`, Keychains, Documents, Desktop, Pictures, Movies,
  Music, iCloud Drive, CloudStorage, Mail, Messages (Large & Old Files and Space Lens only touch what you pick, and
  only to the Trash — in iCloud Drive itself only **Remove Download** of a fully uploaded file, never a delete nor a move; Smart Scan moves stale `node_modules` and build folders of projects in Documents and Desktop to the Trash). Never another volume mounted inside your home (e.g. a connected iPhone's file system in
  `~/Library/Developer/CoreDevice/DeviceFS`, a network share): it is neither measured nor removed, and no module moves to the Trash or deletes a folder that has one mounted inside it. Never passwords, bookmarks, extensions, sign-ins, settings or AI memory.
- **Checked where it is removed.** Smart Scan and Privacy open the folder holding each item without following a symlink, check the safety rules on that folder's real location, make sure the item is still the one the scan found (same file on the same volume) and delete it inside that open folder; every move to the Trash (Smart Scan, Large & Old Files, Space Lens, Uninstaller, Login Items) gets the same check and also holds the item open from the check to the move, moves it by its real location (no symlink on the way), and afterwards makes sure what reached the Trash is that same item. A folder deleted is emptied the same way, one open subfolder at a time: a symlink inside is removed, never followed, and another volume mounted inside is never entered. An item swapped for a symlink or another item since the scan (or between the check and the move) is left alone and logged as **Skipped: changed since the scan**; if something else was swapped in during the move itself, it is not counted as freed, has no Undo and is logged as **Failed** (look for it in the Trash).
- **Exclusions** (Settings › Exclusions): nothing inside an excluded folder, nor an excluded file, is ever listed or removed by any module, and its parent folders are never removed either. Add folders with **Add Folder…**, drag folders or files from Finder into the list, or right-click a path in an expanded Smart Scan/Privacy item, a Large & Old Files row or a Space Lens row and choose **Never Clean This** (packages such as .app and symlinks can't be excluded). The row then leaves Smart Scan and Large & Old Files; Space Lens keeps showing it but won't move it to the Trash. Tool commands (npm, pnpm, uv, go, brew, simctl) can't honor exclusions, so a Smart Scan item cleaned by command whose folder contains, is inside or is an excluded folder is shown unticked and locked with **Blocked by a folder in Exclusions** and a button to the Exclusions settings. Excluding your home folder, `/` or a folder that holds them asks first (**Add Anyway**), since CleanMate would then clean nothing; **Never Clean This** never excludes them.
- **Running apps** are skipped; quit them first. Caches and temporary files that a running process has open are skipped too (checked again while cleaning). Old sessions of Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI and Copilot CLI are also skipped while that command-line tool is running (detected by process name, and by install path for npm installs and wrapper scripts, e.g. `node …/@anthropic-ai/claude-code/cli.js`).
- **Only your user account.** No administrator rights, no privileged helper, no system files. The one exception is moving an app owned by the system or another user to the Trash, where macOS itself asks for the password, as in Finder.
- **Activity log.** Every removed item is recorded (date, module, item, count, size, deleted/trashed/evicted/restored — evicted = download removed in Large & Old Files, the file still in iCloud Drive, counted as freed; restored = put back by Undo, no longer counted as freed; Maintenance tasks add ran/failed with no size; an item that changed since the scan adds skipped with no size, an item left in place adds failed; both with the reason) in
  `~/Library/Logs/CleanMate/activity.log` (rotated at 1 MB), viewable in Settings › Activity as a table (newest first; filter by module or search item names and paths), which also totals the space freed (last 30 days and overall; items moved to the Trash are counted separately).
  Module and item names are stored in English and shown in the current app language.

## Permissions

- **Full Disk Access** (optional): needed to read the Trash (also on external drives), Safari data and some app containers. The welcome
  guide offers it at first launch (Help › Getting Started… shows it again); you can skip it and grant it later in System Settings › Privacy & Security ›
  Full Disk Access.
- Without Full Disk Access, Large & Old Files and Space Lens do not look inside the folders macOS guards with a
  per-folder prompt (Desktop, Documents, Downloads, Movies, Music, Pictures, iCloud Drive, CloudStorage, other apps'
  containers — list in [`ProtectedFolders.swift`](../CleanMate/Engine/ProtectedFolders.swift)), so no access dialogs pop up; a note with an Open Settings
  button says some folders were skipped.
- **App Sandbox is off**: a cleaner must read and remove caches of other apps and developer tools across your home
  folder, which a sandboxed app cannot do. Hardened Runtime is on. CleanMate is distributed outside the Mac App Store
  (Developer ID, notarized).

## Shortcuts and Spotlight

CleanMate adds four actions to the Shortcuts app; Spotlight and Siri offer them too, without making a shortcut
("Scan junk with CleanMate", "Free space in CleanMate"…; in Vietnamese "Quét rác bằng CleanMate"…).
Settings › Integrations describes them and has **Open Shortcuts**.

- **Scan for Junk** scans like Smart Scan, with the Scan Groups and Exclusions set in CleanMate, and reports the junk
  that would be cleaned (items ticked by default, not in use or excluded): the total, the item count and a line per
  group. **Group** limits it to one group (System Junk, Trash, Developer Junk, AI Tools, Applications), even one turned
  off in Settings. Without Full Disk Access the result says some items were not scanned. Nothing is ticked, cleaned or
  saved: it is the same scan as `cleanmate scan`, and Smart Scan's last scan does not change.
- **Clean Selected Junk** opens Smart Scan with **Review Before Cleaning** for the items ticked, scanning first when
  there are no results. It never cleans: you confirm in the review as after **Clean…**. Nothing ticked, a clean running
  or a scan stopped → no review opens.
- **Free Space** reports the free space and size of the disk holding your home folder and the junk the last Smart
  Scan found.
- **Open Module** opens CleanMate on any module.

The results can be passed to the next action: Scan for Junk gives **Junk in Bytes**, **Items**, **Summary** and
**Full Disk Access**; Free Space gives **Free Bytes**, **Capacity in Bytes**, **Junk Found by the Last Scan in Bytes**
and **Summary**. Texts follow CleanMate's language. CleanMate has no scheduled cleaning: a shortcut run by an
automation can scan or report, but cleaning always waits for you. Scan for Junk and Free Space start CleanMate if it
is not running.

## Command line

CleanMate.app includes `cleanmate`, a command with no window for scripts and AI agents (Claude Code, Codex…). It
uses the same items, safety rules, **Settings › Exclusions** and **Scan Groups** as Smart Scan, and reads your ticks.
`list`, `scan`, `space`, `large`, `dupes` and `similar` are read-only. `clean` removes what Smart Scan would, through the app's own
cleaning code, and writes the app's activity log. `trash` moves the files and folders you name to the Trash and
`restore` puts them back. The command changes no setting, reads stdin only to ask before a clean or a move on a
terminal, and never connects to the network.

The command is in the app bundle. Installed with Homebrew (`brew install --cask`), it is already on your PATH: the
cask links it into Homebrew's `bin`, and `brew uninstall` removes the link. Otherwise link it into a folder on your
PATH (no administrator password needed):

```sh
mkdir -p ~/.local/bin   # add it to PATH in your shell profile if it isn't there
ln -s /Applications/CleanMate.app/Contents/Helpers/cleanmate ~/.local/bin/cleanmate
```

| Command | What it prints |
| --- | --- |
| `cleanmate list [--json] [--privacy]` | Every Smart Scan item: group, id, name, what it removes, ticked by default or not, deleted or moved to the Trash, whether it may hold data, whether it needs Full Disk Access. |
| `cleanmate scan [--only ID,ID…] [--group G,G…] [--privacy] [--json] [--require-fda]` | What Smart Scan would find now: items by group with their size, the total ticked by default, and a note when Full Disk Access is missing. |
| `cleanmate clean [--only ID,ID…] [--group G,G…] [--privacy] [--dry-run] [--yes] [--json] [--require-fda]` | Scans again, then cleans; per item what was deleted, moved to the Trash or kept and why, then the totals. |
| `cleanmate space [PATH] [--limit N] [--json]` | What fills a folder, like Space Lens: its children (folders and files) by size, biggest first, the folder's total, and the folders not read without Full Disk Access. Another volume mounted inside (the startup disk's data volume in /System/Volumes, a drive, a network share) is listed as a mounted volume, never measured nor counted in the total, as in Space Lens, so `cleanmate space /` adds up to the startup disk's used space. PATH defaults to your home folder. |
| `cleanmate dupes [--min-size SIZE] [--in PATH] [--limit N] [--json]` | Duplicate files among those `large` finds, like the Duplicates Only filter of Large & Old Files: groups by space wasted, each copy with its last change and what removing it frees. |
| `cleanmate similar [PATH…] [--limit N] [--json]` | Photos that look alike, like Similar Photos in Large & Old Files: groups by space to free, each photo with its size, dimensions and last change, the one suggested to keep first. PATH defaults to your home folder. |
| `cleanmate large [--min-size SIZE] [--older-than DAYS] [--in PATH] [--limit N] [--json]` | Large files, like Large & Old Files: biggest first, with kind, last change, last use, whether other hard links exist and the iCloud Drive state, then the count and total found. |
| `cleanmate trash [--yes] [--json] [--] PATH…` | Moves exactly those files and folders to the Trash, with the safety rules of Space Lens; per path where it went in the Trash, or why it was skipped, then the total. |
| `cleanmate restore [--json] [--] TRASHED…` | Puts back items `trash` moved, named by their place in the Trash, at their old place; never overwrites. |
| `cleanmate --version`, `cleanmate --help` | The app's version; usage, options, Undo, Full Disk Access and exit codes. |

- `--only` scans only the named items (ids from `cleanmate list`); `--group` only the named groups: `system`, `user`
  (the Trash), `dev`, `ai`, `apps`, `privacy`. Both take comma-separated values, may repeat, and accept `--only=…`.
  A group turned off in Scan Groups is scanned only when named.
- Privacy items (browser history, cookies, recent items) are listed and scanned only with `--privacy`.
- An item whose app or tool is running is **locked**, with the reason (`"Google Chrome is running"`), as in the app;
  files a program has open are skipped. Locked items are not counted in the total.
- Docker volumes and stopped containers (`docker-volumes`, `docker-containers`) are deleted for good with what is
  inside them (databases, files written in a container), so `list` and `scan` mark them `"dataLoss": true` (text:
  "may hold data") and `clean` takes them only when named with `--only`. Named, every volume or container listed is
  removed, one by one, except locked rows: the rows you tick in the app are not seen by the command, so to keep some,
  clean them in Smart Scan instead.
- Text output has colours only on a terminal and never when `NO_COLOR` is set; `--json` prints one JSON object.
  Names and descriptions are always in English.
- Exit codes: `0` OK (also when items or files were skipped), `1` error, `2` invalid arguments or a PATH that does not
  exist, is not a folder or can't be read, `3` no Full Disk Access with `--require-fda`, or a `space` / `large` PATH in a
  folder macOS protects without it, `4` `clean` or `trash` without `--yes` and without a terminal (nothing was
  deleted or moved).

Each item lists its 50 largest entries (paths in your home start with `~/`) and `moreEntries` counts the rest. An item

and reports `"fullDiskAccess": false`; `--require-fda` exits with code 3 instead. To grant it: System Settings ›
Privacy & Security › Full Disk Access, turn on that app, then quit and reopen it.

### What takes space

`cleanmate space`, `cleanmate large`, `cleanmate dupes` and `cleanmate similar` answer "what fills my disk?" with the
engines of **Space Lens** and **Large & Old Files**, so a script or an agent doesn't need `du`, `find` or `md5`. All four only read: to
remove what they find, use `cleanmate trash` (below), or Space Lens or Large & Old Files in the app.

- `space` measures each child of PATH (default: the home folder; `~/…` and relative paths work) like Space Lens: hard
  links counted once, files on other volumes not counted. `--limit N` (default 20) shows the N biggest; `moreItems`
  and `moreSize` count the rest. Run it again on the biggest folder to drill down.
- `large` finds files of `--min-size` or more (default `50MB`; accepts `KB`, `MB`, `GB`, `TB`, 1 MB = 10⁶ bytes) in
  the home folder or the folder given with `--in`, with the same rules as Large & Old Files: **Settings › Exclusions**,
  nothing in `~/Library` but iCloud Drive's downloaded files (home folder, with Full Disk Access), hidden folders, app
  bundles and other packages, `node_modules`, `Pods` or `DerivedData`; an external drive works. A file that syncs with
  iCloud Drive has `icloud`: `uploaded`, `notUploaded`, `syncing` or `conflict`. `--min-size` is at least 50 MB for the whole home folder and 1 MB for a folder.
  `--older-than DAYS` keeps files not opened (per Spotlight) or changed for that long. `--limit N` (default 50) shows
  the N biggest; `count` and `size` cover all.
- `dupes` finds identical files among the files `large` finds (same `--in`, `--min-size` and rules), like the
  Duplicates Only filter: files of equal size compared by SHA-256, a hard-linked file counted once (never a copy of
  itself), APFS clones that share their blocks freeing nothing. Groups come most `wasted` first (what moving every copy
  but the oldest to the Trash frees); each copy has `path`, `modified`, `freeable` and `oldest`. `--limit N` (default
  20) groups; `count` and `wasted` cover all. To remove copies, pass the ones to drop to `cleanmate trash`, or use
  the Duplicates Only filter of Large & Old Files.
- `similar` finds photos that look alike in each PATH (default: the home folder; `--in PATH` works too), like Similar
  Photos: JPEG, HEIC, PNG, TIFF and WebP files of 20 KB or more, compared on this Mac with Vision, with the same rules
  as `large` (**Settings › Exclusions**, never a Photos library, an app or `~/Library`). Groups come most `freeable`
  first; each photo has `path`, `size`, `width`, `height`, `modified` and `suggested` (the one to keep: most pixels,
  then largest, then newest, listed first). Nothing is chosen for you: pass the photos you want gone to
  `cleanmate trash`, and `cleanmate restore` puts them back. It reads the app's cache of photos already analyzed but
  never writes it, so the first run on many photos takes a while. `--limit N` (default 20) groups; `count` and
  `freeable` cover all. `scripts/bench-similar.sh` (in the source repository) times it on 2,000 generated photos
  (`-p` for more), with its peak memory and how many of the generated groups it finds.
- Without Full Disk Access, the folders macOS protects (Desktop, Documents, Downloads, Mail…) are never opened, so no
  permission prompt appears: they are listed in `skipped` (text: a note) and, in `space`, as items with `"skipped": true`
  and `size` `null`. A PATH inside one exits with code 3.

### Cleaning

`cleanmate clean` cleans like **Clean** in Smart Scan:

- **What**: the items named with `--only`, ticked or not; otherwise the items ticked by default (your own ticks
  included), in the groups named with `--group` if any. Privacy items only when named, by id or with
  `--group privacy`, and always with `--privacy`. Items that may hold data (`"dataLoss": true`: Docker volumes and
  stopped containers) only when named with `--only`: ticked in the app, they are reported `locked` with the reason
  `"May hold data: name it with --only docker-volumes to clean it"` and left alone, because a tick you made while
  looking at the list in the app is not an OK for a script or an agent. Run `cleanmate scan` first to see them.
- **How**: junk apps rebuild (caches, logs, developer junk, privacy traces) is deleted; items `cleanmate list` marks
  `"disposal": "trash"` go to the Trash. Every path passes the same checks as in the app right before it goes: the
  safety rules, Exclusions, files in use and anything changed since the scan. Running apps are read again: an item
  whose app is running is not cleaned and is reported `locked` with the reason.
- **Confirmation**: on a terminal, `clean` lists the items with their size (deleted or to the Trash) and asks
  `Proceed? [y/N]`. Without a terminal (scripts, AI agents) it deletes nothing and exits with code `4` unless you pass
  `--yes`. `--dry-run` needs neither: it prints the same report, deletes nothing and logs nothing.
- **Activity log**: each cleaned item gets the same line as a clean in the app (module Smart Scan, or Privacy), so
  **Settings › Activity** and the Overview count it.
- **Undo**: deleted junk can't be put back (the apps that use it rebuild it). Items moved to the Trash are listed by
  `cleanmate restore --list` and put back with `cleanmate restore` (see [Moving files to the Trash](#moving-files-to-the-trash)).

`cleanmate clean --yes --only user-caches,old-downloads --json` (bytes; `freed` counts what was deleted or moved to the
Trash, `inTrash` the part in the Trash, `upTo` the part from items whose size is only a maximum; `log` is `null` with
`--dry-run`):

### Moving files to the Trash

`cleanmate trash PATH…` moves exactly the files and folders named to the Trash (`~/…` and relative paths work; no
wildcards, no recursion beyond a folder named whole). Nothing is ever deleted.

- **Rules**: the same as moving an item to the Trash in Space Lens. Each path must be inside your home folder (also with
  the symlinks of its folders resolved), not in `~/Library`, not hidden or inside a hidden folder (`~/.ssh`,
  `~/.gnupg`), not inside an app or package, not a top-level folder of the home (`~/Documents` itself) nor the home
  folder, on the home's volume, not excluded in **Settings › Exclusions** (nor a folder holding an exclusion), and not
  a symlink itself (name the file it points to). Without Full Disk Access, a path in a folder macOS protects is
  skipped without being read. Anything refused is reported `"skipped": true` with the `reason` and not moved.
- **How**: each item is checked again right before it moves, through the same open folder, so an item swapped for a
  symlink or another item since the check is not moved ("Changed while it was checked"). A folder is measured like
  Space Lens (hard links once). A file locked in Finder is skipped.
- **Confirmation**: on a terminal, `trash` lists the paths with their size and asks `Proceed? [y/N]`. Without a
  terminal (scripts, AI agents) it moves nothing and exits with code `4` unless you pass `--yes`.
- **Activity log**: each item moved is logged `trashed` under the module **Command Line**; `restore` logs `restored`,
  which **Settings › Activity** takes off the total in the Trash.
- **What can be put back**: every move to the Trash by CleanMate is recorded, also after CleanMate quit: `cleanmate
  trash` and `clean`, and in the app Smart Scan, Large & Old Files, Space Lens and the Uninstaller. The record (old
  place, place in the Trash, size, date, module) is kept in `~/Library/Application Support/CleanMate/trashed.json`
  until the item is put back or leaves the Trash: every item of the last 20 moves, up to 20,000 items, and the newest
  move always whole. An unreadable file is set aside as `trashed.json.broken-<date>` and a new one started. The item
  itself is never copied. Junk that was
  deleted has no record. Items you moved in Finder are put back with **Put Back** in Finder.
- **List**: `cleanmate restore --list` shows the recorded items still in the Trash, newest first, at most 200
  (`--limit N`): date, size, old place, module and place in the Trash. It only reads, and creates no file.
- **Restore**: `cleanmate restore TRASHED…` takes paths in the Trash, as `trash` or `restore --list` printed them
  (`trashed`), and puts each item back at its old place, only when nothing is there now (never overwrites), its folder
  is still the same folder (not moved, removed or replaced by a symlink, on the same volume) and it is still the item
  CleanMate moved. It logs `restored` under the module that moved it, so **Settings › Activity** takes it off that
  module's total in the Trash.
- `--` ends the options: every argument after it is a path, even one starting with `-`.

### For AI agents

The [Agent Skill](https://agentskills.io) in [`skills/cleanmate/`](../skills/cleanmate/SKILL.md) tells Claude Code,
Codex, Cursor and other agents how to use `cleanmate`: find it, scan, show you the result by group (deleted or to the
Trash, locked items and why, Full Disk Access), clean with `--yes --only` only the items you confirmed, then report what
was freed, moved to the Trash and kept. Asked what takes space, it runs `cleanmate space` (then again on the biggest
folder), `cleanmate large`, `cleanmate dupes` or `cleanmate similar` and shows the result. To remove what it found, it runs
`cleanmate trash --yes` only with the exact paths you confirmed in the conversation, and tells you `cleanmate restore`
puts them back. It never installs anything, never adds Privacy items or items that are not ticked by default unless
you ask, cleans an item that may hold data (Docker volumes, stopped containers) only when you name it after seeing its
list, never deletes or moves files by hand, and points you to the app for what the command doesn't do (Uninstaller,
Maintenance). Install it with:

```sh
bunx skills add homielab/cleanmate-macos
```

or copy the `skills/cleanmate` folder into your agent's skills folder (`~/.claude/skills/`, `.cursor/skills/`…). A test
(`AgentSkillTests`) checks that every command in the skill parses, uses options `cleanmate --help` lists and real item
ids, and that its JSON samples decode as the command's reports.

## Privacy

CleanMate collects no data: no analytics, no telemetry. Its only network connection is the update check, done with
[Sparkle](https://sparkle-project.org):

- **CleanMate › Check for Updates…** checks now. Automatic checks are off on the first launch; on the second launch
  CleanMate asks (unless you already chose in the welcome guide) whether to check automatically (and whether to download and install updates by itself).
- **Settings › General › Updates**: **Automatically check for updates** and **Automatically download and install
  updates** (only with automatic checks on) change that answer at any time.
- A check downloads the update feed (`appcast.xml`) from this project's latest GitHub Release; the update itself is
  downloaded only when you install it or allow automatic downloads. No system profile or other data is sent.
- Every update is signed with the project's EdDSA key and checked before it is unpacked; macOS checks its Developer ID
  signature and notarization as for the first install.

The scan reminder, the low disk warning and **Freed …** after a clean are local notifications posted by macOS on your Mac: they use no network.

**Help › Report a Bug…** sends nothing either: it shows you the report first, then opens GitHub in your browser, where
you choose whether to submit it.

Settings are stored in the app's own preferences on your Mac.
