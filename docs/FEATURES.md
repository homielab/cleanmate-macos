## Modules

| Module | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Overview** | The page CleanMate opens on; read-only, nothing to tick or remove. **Drives**: the startup disk, then each external drive mounted in /Volumes that is local and writable (network shares, read-only disk images and Time Machine backup disks are left out), with used / total, free space as Finder counts it (the same figure as the menu bar item) and a capacity bar; past 90 % used the bar turns orange (red past 95 %) with **Almost full**. **Free space over time**: a chart of the startup disk's free space over the last 30 days (hover a day for its date and free space) and a sentence on where it is heading: losing or gaining about so much a week (losing also says in how many weeks the disk is full at that rate) or stable. Free space is recorded once a day, the latest reading of the day kept, when CleanMate starts and when Smart Scan finishes a scan or a clean; the last 400 days are kept in `~/Library/Application Support/CleanMate/disk-history.tsv`. The chart shows after readings on two days. **Freed by CleanMate**: three cards with what was deleted in the last 30 days (the menu bar's figure), in total, and in the last cleanup with its date, from the activity log, and under them a bar chart of what was deleted on each of the last 30 days (hover a bar for its date and size); items moved to the Trash are not counted. The first time the Overview shows after CleanMate starts, the numbers count up and the drive bars and both charts grow into place (under a second, once; with **Reduce motion** on they only fade in); after **Refresh**, changed figures roll to their new value. **Space outside your folders**: space in use that no folder in the list explains, for information only (local snapshots are deleted in Maintenance). **Time Machine local snapshots**: how many copies of the disk Time Machine keeps on the Mac between backups, and from which dates to which, listed with `tmutil listlocalsnapshots /` (no administrator password); their size can't be measured without administrator rights, so none is shown. macOS deletes them by itself when it needs the space; **Delete…** opens Maintenance, where they can be deleted now. **Purgeable space**: what macOS frees when it needs room (downloaded iCloud files, snapshots, system caches), already counted as free; shown from 100 MB. **System and apps**: used space on the startup disk minus your measured home folder (macOS, apps, other users, local snapshots); without Full Disk Access it also holds the skipped folders, with a note. **Largest folders in your home**: the 12 biggest folders directly in your home, hidden ones and ~/Library included, measured in the background like Space Lens (without Full Disk Access, Desktop, Documents, Downloads, Movies, Music and Pictures are left out, with a note); click one to open it in Space Lens, which then shows it without measuring again. **System protection**: whether Gatekeeper, the firewall, System Integrity Protection and FileVault are on (green), off (orange) or could not be read (grey, **Unknown**), and the XProtect version with the date it was installed. They are read in the background with the status commands macOS ships (`spctl --status`, `socketfilterfw --getglobalstate`, `csrutil status`, `fdesetup status`; no administrator password, no network, a few seconds at most each) and from XProtect's Info.plist. When one is off, a sentence says what that means and **Open System Settings…** opens the pane where it is turned on (SIP is changed only from macOS Recovery). CleanMate shows these settings only; it never changes them and gives no score. **Refresh** (or ⌘R) reads everything again; after a cleanup the folders are measured again the next time the Overview is shown, and the protections are read again each time it is shown. |
| **Smart Scan** | System junk, developer junk, AI tool data and app caches in one scan — see [Smart Scan in detail](#smart-scan-in-detail). Items (simulator data too) are measured in parallel, so a scan finishes sooner. |
| **Privacy** | Per browser (Safari, Chrome, Edge, Brave, Arc, Opera, Vivaldi, Firefox, and the Beta/Dev/Canary channels of Chrome and Edge and Brave Beta/Nightly, each its own group shown only when installed and skipped while that channel runs): browsing history, download history, cookies, autofill form data (Firefox history and downloads are erased inside places.sqlite; bookmarks are kept; Safari history is skipped while Safari or a macOS service still has its database open). System: recent items, QuickLook thumbnails, shell / REPL / less / Vim history, saved Terminal and iTerm2 session contents (skipped while the app runs; iTerm2 hidden when not installed or empty). Nothing is ticked by default. |
| **Large & Old Files** | Files ≥ 50 MB in your home folder — or only in the **Scope** you pick in the toolbar: Downloads, Desktop, Documents, each mounted external drive (listed by name), any folder inside your home or on an external drive via **Choose Folder…** (other folders outside home, folders in ~/Library and hidden folders are refused), or one of the last 5 chosen; on an external drive, hidden folders (its .Trashes, Spotlight index) and other drives mounted inside are skipped, and files go to that drive's own Trash; the scope is remembered between launches (a folder that is gone or no longer allowed falls back to your home folder); changing it scans again; a folder scope can also go down to **At least 1 MB** or **10 MB** (choosing a smaller size scans again, a larger one only filters; the home folder stays at 50 MB or more), and without Full Disk Access a folder macOS protects shows a request for access instead of being scanned — filtered by size, last use (Spotlight's last-opened date, else last access; a later modification also counts) and kind (video, archive, disk image, installer…), a toolbar search by file name or folder, a **Duplicates Only** filter (files with an identical copy, grouped together with a Copies column; only files of the same byte size are read and compared by SHA-256 (files whose beginning already differs are not read in full, and files are read a piece at a time, so it uses little memory even on very large files; the progress bar always reaches 100%, even if some files can't be read; hashes are remembered between launches, even when you stop the search midway, so unchanged files are not read again), hard links to one file count once; APFS clones (Finder's Duplicate, `cp -c`) are listed with their group, labelled **APFS clone — moving it frees no space** (or **frees only** the part they no longer share), and the selected size counts only what they free, so a group of clones shows 0 bytes; nothing is ticked for you; **Select Extra Copies** ticks every copy except one per group and except APFS clones: the oldest by modification date, ties go to the shortest path, counting copies hidden by other filters too; if every copy of a file is ticked, the Trash confirmation warns that none will be left), a sortable Location column (containing folder, `~` for home), Quick Look with Space, **Select All**/**Deselect All** for the files the filters show, **Show in Space Lens** in the right-click menu. A file that has other hard links is marked **Hard link — moving it frees no space**. Selected files go to the Trash (⌘⌫ or the button, after a confirmation). Folders are read in parallel, so a scan takes about half as long. |
| **Uninstaller** | Apps in /Applications and ~/Applications (including up to three subfolders down, e.g. `Adobe Photoshop 2025/`; never helper apps inside another app) with their data in ~/Library (Application Support, Caches, Preferences, Containers…), plus leftovers of apps already removed — also folders in `~/.config`, `~/.local/share` and `~/.local/state` that no installed app, command-line tool (in your PATH, the `PATH` set in your shell profile, or the usual bin folders) or Homebrew formula/cask is named after (marked "No app with this name is installed", never ticked, to the Trash; a CLI's own folders such as `claude`, `codex`, `gemini` and shared ones such as `fonts` are never listed). Data is matched by bundle id, a group container named `<TEAMID>.<group>` also by the team that signed the app (read from the selected app's code signature; listed as **Possibly related** instead when another installed app of the same vendor, or signed by the same team, may share it — telling that reads the other installed apps' signatures once per session, about a second for 120 apps, only when such a container is found; never a container of another release channel of the app or of another installed app's bundle id), and in Application Support, Caches and Logs also by a folder named like the app — its name, its bundle name or executable (`Code` for Visual Studio Code), or that name without spaces or with `-`/`_` (`DiskDrill`, `beekeeper-studio`) — unless another installed app goes by that name too. A folder in Application Support that only starts with the app's first word (`BraveSoftware` for Brave Browser, when no other app starts that way) is listed as **Possibly related**, and the app's config outside ~/Library — `~/.config/<name>`, `~/.local/share/<name>`, `~/.local/state/<name>`, `~/.<name>` or `~/.<name>-…` (`~/.config/zed`, `~/.local/state/zed`, `~/.cursor`) — is listed too; neither is ticked for you. Config is never shell or login files, keys (`.ssh`, `.gnupg`, `.aws`, `.kube`), `~/.config`, `~/.cache`, `~/.local`, `~/.local/share` or `~/.local/state` themselves (nor anything through a symlinked one), stores shared by developer tools (`.npm`, `.cargo`, `.android`…), a command-line tool's own config (`~/.claude`, `~/.gemini`…), nor a name used by a command-line tool installed outside the app. Each app row shows the app's size and, once measured in the background (all apps at once, so it fills in quickly), the size of its data ("45 MB · data 1.2 GB", the same files its detail lists, unticked ones included, except group containers matched only by the app's team; without Full Disk Access Containers and Group Containers are not counted). Sort the list by Name, Size (largest first, app + data once measured) or Last Used (oldest first) from the toolbar, and show only apps not used for over 3 months, 6 months or a year (it combines with the search); both choices are remembered. When macOS has no last-opened date for an app, an open app counts as used today; otherwise the row shows an estimate ("Last used ≈ 3 days ago") from when its preferences, cache or Application Support folder last changed, or **Unknown** when there is none. Unknown apps sort last and are left out of the "not used for" filter; a note below the list says how many and **Show** lists them too. Drag an app onto the window to select it. Tick the checkbox of several apps (the **Applications** header ticks every app listed, for example after **Not Used for a Year**) to uninstall them together: the panel lists the ticked apps, each with its size (the app plus its data surely its own) and **Show Files** to review it alone, and **Uninstall N Apps…** asks once, naming them. Each app then goes to the Trash with its ticked data only — **Possibly related** folders and config are never part of a batch; review an app on its own to remove those. An open app is skipped (not counted in the size), and an app locked in Finder or one that can't go to the Trash is left with its reason in the result; neither stops the others. One Undo puts back everything moved. Ticks stay while you search, filter or look at one app. An app installed with `brew install --cask` (found in Homebrew's Caskroom, without running brew) shows **Homebrew cask (<token>)**; uninstalling it moves the app to the Trash like any app, then runs `brew uninstall --cask <token>` (no `--zap`, no `--force`, no auto-update) so Homebrew drops its record and `brew upgrade` doesn't put the app back. If brew fails or isn't installed, the app stays in the Trash and the result says to run `brew uninstall --cask <token>` in Terminal; the attempt is recorded in **Settings › Activity** either way. While a `brew` command runs, a cask app is not uninstalled (try again when it finishes). Undo puts back only the app; Homebrew no longer lists it (`brew install --cask --adopt <token>` takes it back). **Reset…** (next to **Uninstall…**) fixes a misbehaving app without reinstalling it: a sheet lists the app's own data in ~/Library (caches, preferences, Application Support, containers and group containers, saved state, logs, WebKit and HTTP storage) with sizes, all ticked; untick any to keep it. Only those go to the Trash; the app itself, its launch agents and login items, "Possibly related" folders and config outside ~/Library are never part of a reset. The button is off while the app is open (quit it first). Unticking the app's own row does the same from the detail list. Right-click a data row and choose **Show in Space Lens** to see it in its folder. Everything goes to the Trash. An app owned by the system or another user (for example from the App Store) goes to the Trash the way Finder does it: macOS asks for an administrator password; if you cancel, nothing is moved and its data stays (putting such an app back with Undo may not be permitted — it then stays in the Trash). **Unknown Hidden Config** lists hidden items in your home folder and items in `~/.config` that no known tool, installed command-line tool or app explains, flagging ones that contain a program/script or changed in the last 7 days — warning only, CleanMate never removes them (Reveal in Finder / Quick Look). **Extensions** lists preference panes, Quick Look plug-ins, services, screen savers, input methods, internet plug-ins and Spotlight importers in `~/Library` (not `/Library`) with their type, size, date and owning app (same bundle id or vendor), marking ones whose app is gone as **App removed** (apps open from outside the Applications folders count as installed). Nothing is ticked for you: tick items and choose **Move to Trash…**; only an item directly in one of those folders, with the right type (`.prefPane`, `.qlgenerator`, `.workflow`, `.saver`, `.app`/`.bundle` input method, `.plugin`/`.webplugin`, `.mdimporter`), not a symlink and not Apple's is moved (checked again right before), and Undo / ⌘Z puts it back. While System Settings is open, preference panes can't be ticked (**Close System Settings to move**, with a **Quit System Settings** button). |
| **Space Lens** | Browse any folder (**Choose Folder…**; the **Location** menu lists the last 5 chosen under **Recent**) sorted by size (measured like `du`) with a donut chart of its contents (a stacked bar on macOS 13), drill down (breadcrumb from your home folder for folders inside it, ⌘[ to go back), search the current folder by name (the total and donut still cover the whole folder), Quick Look with Space, move items in your home folder to the Trash (⌘⌫ or the button, after a confirmation). The **Location** menu also lists each mounted drive by name: a folder on an external drive opens with the breadcrumb from the drive and is measured on that drive only; on a writable external drive, files and folders go to that drive's own Trash (a folder only when it holds no hidden items besides Finder's `.DS_Store` and `._` files, no packages, links or other drives; hidden items, its .Trashes and the drive itself are never moved), and Undo puts them back. Read-only, network and Time Machine drives are view-only. Big folders are measured in parallel, so a scan of your home folder takes about half as long. |
| **Login Items** | Your launch agents with owner app and state: disable, enable or remove (plist to the Trash). The plist's Label is read again right before; if it changed, can't be read or another plist in the folder uses the same Label, CleanMate refuses instead of touching another service. System agents are read-only. |
| **Maintenance** | Five fixes macOS users run often, plus a Homebrew update, each with a **Run** button, its status (running, done or failed, with the time) and the tools' output under **Output**. **Rebuild Launch Services** removes duplicate and outdated apps from **Open With** menus: it compacts the Launch Services database (`lsregister -gc`) and registers the apps of every domain again (`lsregister -r -f -apps local,system,user`; the old `-kill` option no longer exists). **Reset Quick Look** restarts Quick Look and clears its thumbnail cache (`qlmanage -r`, `qlmanage -r cache`) when previews are blank, wrong or slow. **Restart Finder and Dock** (`killall Finder`, `killall Dock`; macOS starts both again) asks first, because Finder windows close and a copy in progress in Finder stops. **Reset Font Cache** fixes text drawn in the wrong font or as boxes: it removes your own font registry (`atsutil databases -removeUser`; the system one needs root) and restarts the font server (`atsutil server -shutdown`, `-ping`; on macOS 14 and later these do nothing); open apps may need a restart. **Clear Icon Cache** fixes wrong or blank icons in the Dock and Finder: it deletes `com.apple.dock.iconcache`, `com.apple.iconservices` and `com.apple.iconservices.store` in your user cache folder (`getconf DARWIN_USER_CACHE_DIR`; never `/Library/Caches`, which needs root), then restarts the Dock (`killall Dock`), after asking first. **Update and Clean Homebrew** (listed only when Homebrew is installed) runs `brew update`, `brew upgrade --formula` and `brew cleanup --prune=all`, in that order: formulae only, never casks, since a cask upgrade may ask for a password or replace an app that is open. brew gets no input (a step that would ask anything fails instead of waiting), no auto-update after the first step and no hints; the three steps together are stopped after 15 minutes. brew's output shows live while it runs, and a last line says how many formulae were upgraded and how much space the cleanup reported freeing. An error from brew shows in the output and stops the steps after it. While another `brew` command runs (in Terminal, for example), the task is locked with a note until it finishes. One task runs at a time, steps run in order and the first failing step stops the task; each tool is stopped after 60 seconds (Homebrew: see above). None needs an administrator password, none removes your files, and each run adds a line to the activity log (**Ran** or **Failed**, no size). Tasks that need root (`purge`, `mdutil`, `dscacheutil`) are left out on purpose. **Time Machine local snapshots** lists the snapshots of the startup disk by date (`tmutil listlocalsnapshots /`, read each time Maintenance is shown), each with **Delete…**, plus **Delete All…** when there are several. Both ask first: a local snapshot is a backup copy Time Machine keeps on the Mac, and deleting it can't be undone; backups on the Time Machine disk are not affected. Each snapshot is deleted with `tmutil deletelocalsnapshots <date>` (no administrator password; stopped after 120 seconds), one after another; one that fails doesn't stop the others, and the list is read again afterwards. Each deletion adds a **Ran** or **Failed** line with the snapshot's date to the activity log; their size can't be measured without administrator rights, so the freed space is not counted. While snapshots are being deleted no task runs, and the other way round. |

## Settings, shortcuts and Undo

**Settings** (⌘,): optional menu bar item (off by default) showing free space out of the disk's size (as in Finder),
the last Smart Scan (its date and what it found to clean, or what cleaning it freed) and the space freed in the last
30 days, with **Scan Now** (opens Smart Scan and starts a scan; disabled, like ⌘R, while a confirmation is open or
something is being cleaned), **Open CleanMate** and **Quit**; Full Disk Access status, age thresholds (logs, temporary files, AI sessions, installers in Downloads — default 30 days, files in Downloads — default 90 days, both 7 to 365), remembered
choices, low disk warning, scan reminder, updates (see [Privacy](#privacy)), excluded folders, and the activity log.
**Remind me to scan** (Settings › General, Off by default): Weekly or Monthly posts a local notification at 10:00
about a week or a month after you last opened CleanMate or finished a Smart Scan (each launch and each finished
Smart Scan moves it), then keeps repeating while CleanMate goes unused. Clicking it opens Smart Scan without starting
a scan; it is not shown while CleanMate is the app in front. macOS asks for permission the first time you turn it on;
if notifications are off, **Turn On Notifications…** opens System Settings › Notifications. Monthly reminders use
the 28th at the latest, so no month is skipped.
**Warn when free space is below** (Settings › General, 10% by default; Off, 5%, 15% or 20%): a local notification
such as "“Macintosh HD” has 8 GB free (8%)" when the disk holding your home folder (the one the menu bar item shows)
has less free space than that share of its size. It is checked when CleanMate opens, when Smart Scan finishes a scan or
a clean, and every hour while CleanMate runs, also with its window closed (CleanMate does not start at login); at most
one warning a day. Clicking it opens Smart Scan without starting a scan; while CleanMate is the app in front it goes
to Notification Center without a banner. macOS asks for permission the first time a warning is due or you change the
setting; if notifications are off, **Turn On Notifications…** appears in the row. **View** › ⌘1–⌘8 switch modules (⌘1 Overview, then the sidebar order: Smart Scan ⌘2 … Login Items ⌘7, Maintenance ⌘8); **Scan › Scan Again** (⌘R) rescans the visible module and **Scan › Stop** (⌘.) stops a running scan. With VoiceOver, the warning shown when you exclude a broad folder and the confirmation that remembered choices were reset are read aloud, and keyboard focus moves to **Cancel** when a confirmation row appears in Settings. Clean/Trash have no shortcut on purpose, and while a delete or Trash confirmation is open ⌘R, ⌘., ⌘⌫ and ⌘1–⌘8 do nothing.
Before the first scan, Smart Scan shows the free space on your disk and how much CleanMate freed in the last 30 days.
In Smart Scan and Privacy the checkboxes are large and a click anywhere on a row ticks or unticks it (its buttons, links and file paths keep their own clicks); ticked rows are tinted. A locked row shows a lock on its checkbox with the reason below it. Each group header shows its total and a checkbox that ticks every item that can be ticked, unticks them all, or shows a dash when only some are ticked (items that are locked or empty stay unticked); a click anywhere on the header but its arrow does the same, and the arrow collapses the group. Arrow keys move between rows and Space ticks or unticks the selected ones. The Uninstaller's file list (leftovers and Reset too) and Extensions work the same way (rows that can't be ticked stay as they are); in Unknown Hidden Config, Space opens Quick Look. After a scan, items in each group are sorted largest first (during the scan they keep a fixed order so rows don't jump). Rows show the app's icon when there is one and a small bar comparing each size with the largest in the group (Large & Old Files, Uninstaller and Space Lens have the same bars); after cleaning, the result lists what each group freed. "Freed" is the size of what was removed or moved to the Trash (the part in the Trash is shown apart: emptying the Trash frees it), not the change in the disk's free space, which other apps, Spotlight and snapshots change too; the free space before and after is shown below it for reference, with a bar of the disk's used space. The result screen animates in once per cleanup, within a second: the freed number counts up, the tick springs in, the group rows come in one after another with their bars, and the disk bar moves from before to after; screens fade into each other. With **Reduce motion** (System Settings › Accessibility › Display) everything only fades in and the number shows its final value at once.
After a scan the sidebar shows a badge next to Smart Scan and Privacy (size selected) and Large & Old Files (files found). After moving items to the Trash, an **Open Trash** button opens it in Finder. In Large & Old Files, Space Lens, Uninstaller (also a Reset) and Smart Scan an **Undo** button puts the items of that module's last move to the Trash back where they were — only while they are still in the Trash, never over something now in their place, and only if their folder still exists (not replaced by a link, nor by another disk mounted there); anything it can't put back is counted and left in the Trash. Undo lasts until CleanMate quits (nothing is saved). Launch agents an uninstall booted out stay unloaded until the next login. The same undo is also on **Edit › Undo Move to Trash / Undo Uninstall / Undo Reset (⌘Z)**; using either one removes the other, and there is no redo. It is dropped by a new scan, a new move to the Trash, another Space Lens folder, or switching to another module. While typing in a search field, ⌘Z first undoes the typing. In Smart Scan, Undo puts back only the items it moved to the Trash (mail attachments, backups, iOS updates, old installers, old downloads, old screenshots, abandoned downloads, stale node_modules and build folders); what it deleted outright stays deleted.
Items locked in Finder can't be moved to the Trash: Large & Old Files, Space Lens and Extensions count them separately as **Locked in Finder (turn off Locked in Get Info): N** (a folder that only contains locked files still moves). Uninstalling a locked app says so — select it, choose File > Get Info, turn off Locked, then try again — and nothing is moved, its data included.
CleanMate has a single main window; closing it keeps the app running — click the Dock icon (or **Open CleanMate** in the menu bar item) to bring it back. Quitting (⌘Q or the menu bar's Quit) while cleaning, moving items to or back from the Trash, or changing a login item asks first (**Quit Anyway**; Esc or Return cancels): what was done stays done, the rest is left, and there is no Undo.

Every scan shows its progress and can be stopped with **Stop**, which ends it right away even deep inside a large folder tree; cleaning, once started, is never interrupted. If CleanMate is in the background when a scan or clean finishes, its Dock icon bounces once (no notification, no sound; not after Stop). From the second scan on, the progress bar is based on how long each item (Smart Scan, Privacy) or how many files (Large & Old Files) the previous scan took.

## Smart Scan in detail

**System**
- User caches (a folder named after a running app's bundle ID or name is skipped; generic vendor names such as Google or Microsoft don't count; so is a folder holding a file another process has open, e.g. a CLI daemon; Poetry's project virtualenvs in `~/Library/Caches/pypoetry/virtualenvs` are never removed; updates apps downloaded with Sparkle are listed under **Downloaded app updates** instead).
- Caches of sandboxed (App Store) apps inside their containers and of app groups (Group Containers, e.g. Microsoft Office, Slack) — only with Full Disk Access; never Apple's or running apps. An app group named `<TEAMID>.<group>` is kept while any app signed by that team runs (the team is read from the running apps' code signatures, nothing else); a `group.<id>` group, or one while a running app's signature can't be read, is kept while an app of the same vendor runs.
- Copies of attachments opened in Mail — only with Full Disk Access and Mail closed; older than the old-logs age, unticked, moved to the Trash.
- iPhone and iPad backups made by Finder — only with Full Disk Access; unticked, moved to the Trash; each backup listed with its device name and date when expanded, with its own checkbox so you can keep some; skipped while a backup runs or if written to in the last hour.
- Downloaded iOS/iPadOS updates (`.ipsw` in `~/Library/iTunes/iPhone|iPad|iPod Software Updates`, plus Apple Configurator's firmware cache with Full Disk Access) — only `.ipsw` files, each listed with device and version (e.g. "iPhone · 18.1 (22B83)") and its own checkbox; unticked, moved to the Trash; skipped while Finder or Apple Configurator updates a device.
- **macOS installers** — `Install macOS *.app` and `Install OS X *.app` directly in `/Applications` or `~/Applications` (12–15 GB each, left behind after an upgrade or after making a USB installer). Only Apple's real installer apps (bundle ID `com.apple.InstallAssistant…`), never through a symlink, and only those you can move without a password: one owned by root or in a folder you can't write to is not listed, and nothing ever asks for an administrator password. Each one is listed with its name, version and size, with its own checkbox; unticked (you may keep one to install again), moved to the Trash so Undo puts it back. Skipped while an installer runs (the installer app, `startosinstall`, `createinstallmedia`). No Full Disk Access needed. `/Library/Updates`, `/System` and installers in Downloads (**Old installers**) are never touched.
- **Old installers** — only with Full Disk Access: disk images, packages, ISO images, Xcode archives (`.dmg`, `.pkg`, `.iso`, `.xip`) and zip files with an app at their top level (`Name.app/`), directly in `~/Downloads` (never in subfolders, never through a symlink) and older than the installers age (Settings › General, default 30 days). Each file is listed by name and size with its own checkbox; unticked, moved to the Trash. Skipped: a file that is open (such as a mounted disk image) and a download still in progress (`.download`, `.crdownload`, `.part`, `.aria2`, `.!qB` next to it).
- **Old downloads** — only with Full Disk Access: files and folders directly in `~/Downloads` that were not modified, created, added to Downloads or opened (read, or Finder's "Last opened") for the downloads age (Settings › General, default 90 days); a folder only when nothing inside was modified, created or read either. Each one is listed by name, the date it was last touched and size, with its own checkbox; unticked, moved to the Trash, and every date is checked again right before the move. Skipped: hidden files (`.DS_Store`, `.localized`), symlinks, installers (listed under Old installers), a file that is open or a folder holding one, a download still in progress, and a folder with more than 10,000 items, an unreadable folder or a socket inside. Nothing is listed when `~/Downloads` is a symlink.
- **Old screenshots** — only with Full Disk Access: screenshots and screen recordings directly in the folder macOS saves them to (Desktop, or the folder chosen in the Screenshot app's Options › Save to, when it is inside your home folder and not a symlink) that were not modified, created, added or opened for the downloads age (Settings › General, default 90 days). A file counts as a screenshot when the Screenshot app marked it (`kMDItemIsScreenCapture`, read from the file itself) or when its name starts with "Screenshot", "Screen Shot", "Screen Recording", "CleanShot" or the Vietnamese "Ảnh màn hình", "Ảnh chụp màn hình", "Ghi màn hình", "Bản ghi màn hình" and ends in `.png`, `.jpg`, `.jpeg`, `.heic`, `.mov` or `.mp4`. Each one is listed by name, date and size with its own checkbox; unticked, moved to the Trash (Undo puts it back), dates checked again right before the move. Skipped: hidden files, symlinks, files in Exclusions and files that are open. - **Google Drive cache**, **Dropbox cache**, **OneDrive cache & logs**: what cloud sync apps keep locally besides your synced files, often 5–30 GB Finder doesn't show. Google Drive: each account's `content_cache` and the logs in `~/Library/Application Support/Google/DriveFS`, only parts nothing in changed for 7 days, so a file still waiting to upload is kept (files made available offline are downloaded again when opened). Dropbox: `.dropbox.cache` of each Dropbox folder named in `~/.dropbox/info.json` (temporary and recently deleted copies), also in `~/Library/CloudStorage/Dropbox*` with Full Disk Access, and `~/.dropbox/logs`. OneDrive (needs Full Disk Access): the updater's logs and the Caches of its container (not listed again under **Sandboxed app caches**). Unticked and deleted (the apps download again what they need); each is locked while its app, a helper or its Finder extension runs, and files in use are kept. Synced files, sign-ins, settings and the apps' databases (`DriveFS/<account>/*.db`, `metadata_sqlite_db`, `~/.dropbox/instance*`) are never touched. Still, let Google Drive finish uploading before quitting it: a file not uploaded yet may only exist in its cache.
- **Telegram media cache**, **Spotify stream cache**: media these apps keep outside `~/Library/Caches`, often 5–20 GB. Telegram for macOS (needs Full Disk Access): `postbox/media` and `cache` of each account in `~/Library/Group Containers/6N38VWS5BX.ru.keepcoder.Telegram/<channel>/account-*`; the database (`postbox/db`), keys and sign-in are never touched. Spotify: `~/Library/Application Support/Spotify/PersistentCache/Storage` (songs downloaded for offline use are there too and must be downloaded again). Unticked and deleted (the apps download again what they need); each is locked while its app runs, and files in use are kept.
- **Apple Podcasts: downloaded episodes**, **Apple Podcasts cache** (need Full Disk Access): Podcasts keeps downloaded episodes in `~/Library/Group Containers/243LU875E5.groups.com.apple.podcasts/Library/Cache`, often several GB Finder doesn't show. Episodes are the audio and video files directly in that folder, one row each; the cache is the rest of it (artwork, audio cached while streaming). Unticked and deleted: Podcasts downloads an episode again when you play it. Locked while Podcasts runs, and files in use are kept. The library database (`Documents/MTLibrary.sqlite`: subscriptions, played state), settings and downloads in progress (`Library/Caches`) are never touched.

**How it cleans**
- Ticking only some backups, archives, DerivedData projects, device support versions, models or snapshots makes the item's checkbox mixed and its size column shows "selected / total"; those per-entry ticks last until the next scan and every entry is listed (no cap). VoiceOver reads each entry as its label, date and size, not its long path.
- Official tool commands (`npm cache clean`, `brew cleanup`, `go clean`, `simctl`…) are used when installed.
- Sizes are what the command really frees (`brew cleanup --dry-run`, `docker system df`); pnpm has no dry run, and Bun and uv share files with your projects as APFS clones, so their sizes are shown as "Up to …" and kept out of the sure total; after cleaning, the result says how much of what was freed came from them (less space may really be freed). Homebrew's size is reused from the last scan while its cache, Cellar and Caskroom are unchanged (up to 6 hours).
- A file with several hard links in one item is counted once, so sizes match `du -sk`; an item with a subfolder CleanMate can't read says **Some items could not be read — the size may be larger**.
- Files and folders that are locked or protected by macOS (e.g. the empty `com.apple.*` folders in the temporary folder) are never listed: removing them could only fail.

## Safety

- **Preview, then confirm.** Every module lists what it found with sizes; nothing is removed until you confirm.
- **Trash vs delete.** Regenerable data (caches, logs, developer junk) and privacy traces are deleted. User files,
  apps, app data, old AI sessions, old Xcode archives, stale `node_modules` and build folders, and launch agents are moved to the Trash, so you can put them back.
- **Your choices stick.** Items you untick stay unticked on the next scan (Settings › General › Remembered choices › **Reset…**, which asks before forgetting them).
- **Turn off whole groups** (Settings › Scan Groups): a group switched off there (for example Developer Junk or AI Tools) is not scanned nor shown, so Smart Scan finishes sooner; the results say how many groups are off, with an Open Settings button. With every group off, Scan is disabled. Privacy has no such switch (its few browser groups are quick to scan and nothing is ticked by default).
- **Hard blocklist**, checked when scanning and again right before every removal: only paths inside your home or
  temporary folder (plus macOS installer apps in `/Applications`, only moved to the Trash, what you pick on an external drive in Large & Old Files and Space Lens, only moved to that drive's Trash, and your own Trash folder on external drives, which Smart Scan only empties); never `.ssh`, `.gnupg`, `.aws`, `.kube`, Keychains, Documents, Desktop, Pictures, Movies,
  Music, iCloud Drive, CloudStorage, Mail, Messages (Large & Old Files and Space Lens only touch what you pick, and
  only to the Trash; Smart Scan moves stale `node_modules` and build folders of projects in Documents and Desktop to the Trash). Never another volume mounted inside your home (e.g. a connected iPhone's file system in
  `~/Library/Developer/CoreDevice/DeviceFS`, a network share): it is neither measured nor removed, and no module moves to the Trash or deletes a folder that has one mounted inside it. Never passwords, bookmarks, extensions, sign-ins, settings or AI memory.
- **Exclusions** (Settings › Exclusions): nothing inside an excluded folder, nor an excluded file, is ever listed or removed by any module, and its parent folders are never removed either. Add folders with **Add Folder…**, drag folders or files from Finder into the list, or right-click a path in an expanded Smart Scan/Privacy item, a Large & Old Files row or a Space Lens row and choose **Never Clean This** (packages such as .app and symlinks can't be excluded). The row then leaves Smart Scan and Large & Old Files; Space Lens keeps showing it but won't move it to the Trash. Tool commands (npm, pnpm, uv, go, brew, simctl) can't honor exclusions, so a Smart Scan item cleaned by command whose folder contains, is inside or is an excluded folder is shown unticked and locked with **Blocked by a folder in Exclusions** and a button to the Exclusions settings. Excluding your home folder, `/` or a folder that holds them asks first (**Add Anyway**), since CleanMate would then clean nothing; **Never Clean This** never excludes them.
- **Running apps** are skipped; quit them first. Caches and temporary files that a running process has open are skipped too (checked again while cleaning). Old sessions of Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI and Copilot CLI are also skipped while that command-line tool is running (detected by process name, and by install path for npm installs and wrapper scripts, e.g. `node …/@anthropic-ai/claude-code/cli.js`).
- **Only your user account.** No administrator rights, no privileged helper, no system files. The one exception is moving an app owned by the system or another user to the Trash, where macOS itself asks for the password, as in Finder.
- **Activity log.** Every removed item is recorded (date, module, item, count, size, deleted/trashed/restored — restored = put back by Undo, no longer counted as freed; Maintenance tasks add ran/failed with no size) in
  `~/Library/Logs/CleanMate/activity.log` (rotated at 1 MB), viewable in Settings › Activity as a table (newest first; filter by module or search item names and paths), which also totals the space freed (last 30 days and overall; items moved to the Trash are counted separately).
  Module and item names are stored in English and shown in the current app language.

## Permissions

- **Full Disk Access** (optional): needed to read the Trash (also on external drives), Safari data and some app containers. CleanMate offers
  it once at first launch; you can skip it and grant it later in System Settings › Privacy & Security ›
  Full Disk Access.
- Without Full Disk Access, Large & Old Files and Space Lens do not look inside the folders macOS guards with a
  per-folder prompt (Desktop, Documents, Downloads, Movies, Music, Pictures, iCloud Drive, CloudStorage, other apps'
  containers — list in [`ProtectedFolders.swift`](../CleanMate/CleanMate/Engine/ProtectedFolders.swift)), so no access dialogs pop up; a note with an Open Settings
  button says some folders were skipped.
- **App Sandbox is off**: a cleaner must read and remove caches of other apps and developer tools across your home
  folder, which a sandboxed app cannot do. Hardened Runtime is on. CleanMate is distributed outside the Mac App Store
  (Developer ID, notarized).

## Privacy

CleanMate collects no data: no analytics, no telemetry. Its only network connection is the update check, done with
[Sparkle](https://sparkle-project.org):

- **CleanMate › Check for Updates…** checks now. Automatic checks are off on the first launch; on the second launch
  CleanMate asks whether to check automatically (and whether to download and install updates by itself).
- **Settings › General › Updates**: **Automatically check for updates** and **Automatically download and install
  updates** (only with automatic checks on) change that answer at any time.
- A check downloads the update feed (`appcast.xml`) from this project's latest GitHub Release; the update itself is
  downloaded only when you install it or allow automatic downloads. No system profile or other data is sent.
- Every update is signed with the project's EdDSA key and checked before it is unpacked; macOS checks its Developer ID
  signature and notarization as for the first install.

The scan reminder and the low disk warning are local notifications posted by macOS on your Mac: they use no network.

Settings are stored in the app's own preferences on your Mac.

## Languages

English (default) and Vietnamese, following your system language. To use another language for CleanMate only, pick it in
Settings › General › Language (Same as your Mac / English / Tiếng Việt) and click **Restart**; your Mac's language does not change.
